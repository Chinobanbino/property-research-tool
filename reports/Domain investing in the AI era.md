# Start small, buy quality, respect the math

**Bottom line:** in October 2026, a beginner should not start by speculating on AI-themed names. Start with **5–20 carefully researched .com names** (perhaps one or two .ai names later), priced at retail with buy-it-now and lease-to-own options on Afternic and one lower-commission venue. Hold at least two to three years of renewal money in reserve, and treat year one as tuition. The market is healthy at the top. NameBio recorded about **$244M across roughly 190,300 sales in 2025, up 31.9%**, and 1H‑2026 ran another **19.3% ahead** ([NamePros/Bob Hawkes](https://www.namepros.com/blog/2025-domain-name-market-dollar-volume-trends-based-on-namebio-data.1374269/); [NamePros 1H-2026](https://www.namepros.com/blog/analysis-of-domain-name-sales-dollar-volume-reported-at-namebio-during-the-first-six-months-of-2026.1392654/)). AI money drove the record **~$70M AI.com sale** in February 2026 ([TechRadar](https://techradar.com/pro/biggest-ever-website-domain-deal-sees-ai-com-bought-by-crypto-com-founder)). That headline demand sits far above where beginners buy. Generic "AI" registrations peaked in March 2026 and have stalled ([Hostinger](https://www.hostinger.com/blog/domain-analysis-2026/)). Passive portfolios sell only **1–2% of names per year** ([Domain Name Wire](https://domainnamewire.com/2024/07/02/my-no-good-first-half-of-2024/)). Marketplace commissions have risen to roughly 10–30%. Most beginners lose money or break even for the first one to three years. One action is time-sensitive: **Verisign raises .com wholesale from $10.26 to $10.97 on November 1, 2026 (04:00 UTC)**. Renewing names you are sure you will keep before then locks today's price for up to ten years ([BusinessWire/Verisign](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260423135311/en/); [TLDES](https://tldes.com/pages/com-price-increase-2026)). A note on the evidence: the research team could not load most industry sites directly, so **most figures here come from search-result summaries rather than full page reads**. Verify headline numbers, especially fees and prices, on the primary page before acting on them.

## Records go to AI buyers; beginners compete in the four-figure middle

The public sales record is dominated by .com, and AI capital is now the main source of seven- and eight-figure demand. AI.com's roughly **$70M** sale to Crypto.com's Kris Marszalek, for a personal AI agent product, is the largest publicly disclosed domain deal on record ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/amp/brand-makers/ai-com-sold-for-70-million-in-one-of-the-biggest-domain-deals-ever-89586.htm); [DNJournal](https://dnjournal.com/archive/lowdown/2026/posts/0206-2.htm)). Other buyers point the same way. The AI app builder "Anything" paid **$2M for Anything.com** in 2025 ([DNJournal](https://www.dnjournal.com/archive/domainsales/2025/1225.htm)), and OpenAI acquired Chat.com in late 2024 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most_expensive_domain_names)). DNJournal's Ron Jackson said that even excluding AI.com, reported 1H‑2026 volume ran about **15% ahead of 1H‑2025** ([Domain Name Wire](https://domainnamewire.com/2026/07/27/this-years-top-domain-sales/)).

These headlines tell you little about your own odds. The **typical** transaction is far smaller. Sedo's 2024 data showed an **average sale of $2,345 and a median of $549**, with 69% of sales at a fixed buy-it-now price and 59% in .com ([TheDomains](https://www.thedomains.com/2025/03/18/internetx-and-sedo-release-their-global-domain-report-2025)). GoDaddy's average aftermarket transaction was **$321** in Q1 2025 ([DNW](https://domainnamewire.com/2025/05/01/godaddy-reports-1-2b-revenue-in-q1-2025-domain-aftermarket-sales-rise-5/)). A skilled part-time brandable investor, Efty co-founder Doron Vermaat, sold 38 names in 2025 at a **$4,995 median** and a $9,436 average ([DomainBlog](https://www.domainblog.com/2026/01/02/doron-from-efty-share-his-2025-numbers/)). That low-to-mid four-figure band is a realistic target for good investor inventory. Market growth also comes mostly from **more recorded sales, not higher prices**. .com still holds about **72%** of NameBio dollar volume, down from 74.4%, while ccTLDs led by .ai rose to 16.3% ([NamePros](https://www.namepros.com/blog/2025-domain-name-market-dollar-volume-trends-based-on-namebio-data.1374269/)). NameBio captures only an estimated 5–10% of retail sales, so all of these numbers undercount private deals.

## AI split demand into three markets, and only one is clearly strong

AI changed what buyers want, but not evenly. **Tier one is ultra-premium one-word .com and one-word .ai names bought by well-funded AI companies.** This tier is setting records. **Tier two is generic "X‑AI" keyword names, and it is saturated.** Hostinger's analysis of 6.2 million registrations found AI-related names grew only **1.5% from April to September 2026, against 6.7% for all registrations**, after peaking in March. The theme now ranks 13th of 20 ([Hostinger](https://www.hostinger.com/blog/domain-analysis-2026/)). **Tier three is speculative rotation into new vocabulary and extensions.** "Agent" names grew 14.6% and "agentic" 16.7%. After a September 29 US executive order on "super intelligence", Hostinger customers registered 34,582 .si names in two weeks ([ITNerd](https://itnerd.blog/2026/10/14/guest-post-from-ai-to-agentic-to-si-ai-domain-registrations-stall-as-naming-moves-on/)). End-user demand for these names is unproven ([NamePros](https://www.namepros.com/blog/big-tech-wants-agent-investors-are-buying-si-but-where-are-the-end-users.1402646/)). The tier-three trades look like the crypto-name wave that came before them. Bitcoin-term registrations fell 35.4% over the same period ([Hostinger](https://www.hostinger.com/blog/domain-analysis-2026/)).

Two further shifts matter for what to buy. First, **AI startups increasingly keep "AI" out of their brand names**, as an analysis of 6,420 startup domains found ([get.tech](https://get.tech/blog/how-tech-startups-are-naming-themselves/)). That favors short, distinctive brandables over "[word]AI.com" constructions. Second, **AI answer engines are eroding the SEO value of exact-match keyword domains** and shifting value toward distinctiveness and trust ([Strategic Revenue](https://www.strategicrevenue.com/ai-search-may-be-biggest-shift-in-domain-industry-since-fall-of-exact-match-boom/); [CircleID](https://circleid.com/posts/how-ai-answer-engines-are-quietly-rewriting-what-makes-a-domain-brandable)). No hard data yet measures this effect. The measurable 2025 shock came from a different source. **Google ended AdSense for Domains**, cutting parking revenue to "pennies on the dollar", and Sedo's Q3 2025 revenue fell 66% ([Domain Name Wire](https://domainnamewire.com/2025/12/31/2025-in-review-the-death-of-adsense-for-domains/)). Do not count on parking income to cover renewals. A domain now earns money almost only when it sells.

AI naming tools are now "standard equipment" for founders ([NameStation](https://www.namestation.com/blog/domain-naming-trends-2026)). This cuts against hand-registering brandables. If an LLM suggested a coined name to you, it is probably suggesting similar names to thousands of founders and investors. That pushes margins down on the cheapest inventory. This is an inference from the trend, not a measured effect.

## Extension choice is a carrying-cost decision first

For a real estate investor, the closest analogy is property tax and insurance on a vacant lot: renewals are carrying costs you pay every year until you sell. Extension choice sets that cost.

**.com** costs roughly $10–11 a year to renew today. Pre-increase renewals ran about $9.98–$10.18 at Spaceship, about $10.44–$10.46 at cost at Cloudflare, about $10.67–$11.08 at Porkbun and about $10.88–$10.99 at Dynadot ([domaindetails](https://domaindetails.com/registrars/cheapest); [sidebysidedomains](https://sidebysidedomains.com/blog/cheapest-domain-registrar-renewals-2026.html)). After November 1, the at-cost floor including the $0.20 ICANN fee rises from about $10.46 to **$11.17**. If Verisign takes every allowed increase, wholesale could reach about $13.42 by the end of the current contract. OSIR calls that a ceiling, not a forecast ([OSIR](https://osir.com/en/blog/verisign-com-price-update-2026/)). Cloudflare's at-cost pricing requires Cloudflare nameservers. That conflicts with the marketplace landers and Afternic nameserver discounts discussed below, so Spaceship or Porkbun is the more flexible low-cost choice for a selling portfolio. .net rises to $12.00 on March 1, 2027 ([DNW](https://domainnamewire.com/2026/08/27/verisign-announces-net-price-increase/)).

**.ai** is the strongest non-.com extension. It passed **1 million registrations in January 2026** ([Domain Name Wire](https://domainnamewire.com/2026/01/28/ai-namespace-hits-1-million-domain-names/)), and its aftermarket did about **$22M across 7,335 sales with 27 six-figure deals in 2025** ([TLD Investors](https://tldinvestors.com/2026/01/ai-2025-year-in-review.html)). Holding .ai is expensive, though. On March 5, 2026, wholesale rose to **$160 per mandatory two-year term** ($80 a year, up from $70). The two-year minimum applies at registration and at every renewal ([DNW](https://domainnamewire.com/2026/02/02/ai-domain-name-prices-going-up-20/); [NameSilo](https://www.namesilo.com/blog/en/domain-name-search/ai-domain-registry-rules-pricing-and-renewals)). Retail renewals run from about $75 at Epik to $180 at Gandi ([DomainOffer](https://domainoffer.net/tld/ai/epik); [DomainOffer](https://domainoffer.net/tld/ai/gandi)), roughly **7–15 times a .com**. Price growth in the .ai aftermarket has also slowed to a reported +7.7% a year (vendor source) ([Dynadot](https://www.dynadot.com/blog/domain-name-trends)).

**.io** is losing ground to .ai, and Sedo's own report frames the shift as substitution ([TheDomains](https://www.thedomains.com/2025/03/18/internetx-and-sedo-release-their-global-domain-report-2025)). Y Combinator companies now pick .ai over .io as their second-favorite extension ([NameMesh](https://www.namemesh.com/guides/domain-naming/yc-startup-domain-name-trends)). **New gTLDs** (.store, .online, .site and similar) have thin resale markets and "rarely sell profitably" by vendor accounts ([domaindetails](https://domaindetails.com/kb/domain-investing/common-domain-buying-mistakes)). Their registries can also raise renewal prices with no cap. Identity Digital raised prices on **226 extensions on October 6, 2026** ([Spaceship KB](https://www.spaceship.com/knowledgebase/price-update-on-226-identity-digital-domain-extensions/)).

## The break-even formula decides what you can afford to hold

Sell-through rate (STR) is the share of your portfolio that sells in a year. Treat it like absorption rate in real estate. Industry-wide .com STR is "almost certainly under 1%" ([NamePros](https://www.namepros.com/blog/the-minimum-domain-price-for-profitability.1313195/)). Andrew Allemann of Domain Name Wire, an experienced investor, ran **1.96% in 2023 and an annualized 1.36% in early 2024**, and he calls 1–2% "solid" for passive portfolios ([DNW](https://domainnamewire.com/2024/07/02/my-no-good-first-half-of-2024/)). Tightly curated portfolios claim 3–5%, but that figure comes from vendors ([Pixelworks](https://pixelworksdomains.com/blog/sell-through-rate-the-metric-that-predicts-your-domain-profits)). Price trades against STR. One investor's data showed **1.5% STR at a ~$1,450 average price, falling to 0.43% at ~$4,681** ([NamePros](https://www.namepros.com/threads/sell-through-rates.1193687/)). For your first year or two, plan on **0.5–1%**, because a beginner's names are usually weaker than a veteran's.

The core formula:

> **Required STR = annual carrying cost per name ÷ (average sale price × (1 − commission))**

To include what you paid for each name, add acquisition cost spread over your expected holding period to the numerator: **(renewal + acquisition cost ÷ years held) ÷ net sale price**. NamePros applies the same logic name by name as a "renewal ratio", (net sale value × STR) ÷ annual renewal. It should be **at least 1.0** to justify renewing a name ([NamePros](https://www.namepros.com/blog/how-i-ranked-every-name-in-a-2000-domain-name-portfolio.1383894/)).

| Scenario | Annual cost per name | Avg sale price | Commission | Net per sale | Required STR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hand-reg .com, priced well | $11.50 | $2,000 | 20% | $1,600 | **0.72%** |
| Hand-reg .com, priced low | $11.50 | $1,000 | 25% | $750 | **1.53%** |
| Cheap .com, priced at $200 | $11.50 | $200 | 20% | $160 | **7.2%** |
| Auction .com bought at $300, 5‑yr horizon | $11.50 + $60 = $71.50 | $5,000 | 20% | $4,000 | **1.8%** |
| Same name, priced at $2,000 | $71.50 | $2,000 | 20% | $1,600 | **4.5%** |
| .ai at ~$85/yr | $85 | $2,000 | 20% | $1,600 | **5.3%** |
| .ai at ~$85/yr, premium pricing | $85 | $5,000 | 20% | $4,000 | **2.1%** |

The table makes three points. **Low prices kill hand-reg portfolios.** At $1,000 or less you need an STR above what most passive investors achieve. **Acquisition cost dominates early losses.** A $300 auction name has to sell at a strong retail price to earn back its cost. **.ai works only for names that are clearly top-tier for your skill level**, because the carrying cost demands two to seven times the STR of a .com. At portfolio scale the numbers look like this. 200 .coms at $11.50 cost **$2,300 a year**. At 1% STR (two sales at $2,000, net of 20%), you clear about **$900 before acquisition costs**. At 0.5% (one sale), you **lose about $700**. Domavest's version of the same math is 1,000 names, a $2,000 average price and 20% commission, which needs about 0.6% STR just to cover renewals ([Domavest](https://www.domavest.com/2026/02/the-real-economics-behind-buying-and.html)).

**The November 1 action item, sized honestly.** The increase is about **$0.71 per .com per year**. It adds roughly 0.04 percentage points to required STR at a $2,000 price, which matters at the margin for cheap inventory ([Openprovider](https://www.openprovider.com/blog/com-price-increase-domain-resellers)). Renewals paid before **November 1, 2026 at 04:00 UTC** bill at the current rate for the full term, up to ten years ([TLDES](https://tldes.com/pages/com-price-increase-2026)). Verisign has raised prices 7% in four of the last five years ([Openprovider](https://www.openprovider.com/blog/are-domain-prices-going-up)), so the lock is worth more than one year's $0.71. Before the deadline, do three things. First, **renew your real estate business's own .com domains for multiple years**, because you will never drop them. Second, extend any investment .coms you are certain to keep. Third, *don't* prepay names you may prune, because prepaying locks cash in inventory you may later drop. Do not prepay .io as a hedge either, for the sovereignty reasons in the risk section.

## Marketplaces got pricier in 2026; spread listings across two venues

**Dan.com is gone.** It shut down on June 27, 2025, and was folded into GoDaddy's Afternic ([DomainsDealer](https://domainsdealer.com/blog/what-happened-to-dan-com)). **Afternic** is now the main distribution channel. Its **Fast Transfer** feature lists your name in registrar search results across GoDaddy and partner registrars, so buyers can purchase it instantly ([names.center](https://names.center/blog/afternic-domain-marketplace-guide.html)). The most recent third-party reading of Afternic's pricing page shows a **30% default commission**. Pointing nameservers to GoDaddy cuts it to 20%, and also choosing the Basic plan cuts it to 15% ([domaindetails](https://domaindetails.com/kb/domain-investing/domain-aftermarket-platforms-compared)). Treat this as unverified; older sources say 15–25%. Afternic raised its **lease-to-own (LTO) ceiling from $100K to $5M** at ICA 2026 ([Afternic blog](https://blog.afternic.com/whats-new-q1-2026/)). Longer leases earn commission discounts, for example a 36-month lease cuts a 30% default to 20% ([Afternic LTO guide](https://blog.afternic.com/lease-to-own-guide/)). Afternic also reports that LTO deals averaged **35% higher sale prices than buy-it-now** in H1 2024 ([Afternic blog](https://blog.afternic.com/lease-to-own-asp/)). That is platform self-reporting, but it is the best data available.

| Venue | Seller commission (verify before listing) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Afternic (GoDaddy) | ~30% default; 20% with GoDaddy nameservers; 15% with Basic plan | Widest distribution; LTO up to $5M ([domaindetails](https://domaindetails.com/kb/domain-investing/domain-aftermarket-platforms-compared)) |
| Afternic Custom Checkout Link | 5% ($15 minimum, $99 minimum price) | For buyers you bring yourself; third-party report ([PounceDomains](https://pouncedomains.com/blog/dan-com-alternative)) |
| Sedo | 10% (Sedo-parked fixed price) / 15% standard / 20% via partner network | Stable for years ([Sedo](https://sedo.com/us/sell-domains/it-pays-to-sell-at-sedo)) |
| Spaceship SellerHub | 10% marketplace (up from 5% in Feb 2026); 5% checkout links | Fee can be split with buyer ([DNW](https://domainnamewire.com/2026/02/11/spaceship-increasing-sellerhub-commission-to-10/); [Spaceship](https://www.spaceship.com/en-GB/sellerhub/)) |
| Atom | 2026 schedule not found | Strong for brandables; 3 of the top 10 .ai sales of 2025 ([TLD Investors](https://tldinvestors.com/category/atom)) |
| Escrow.com (direct deals) | ~2.6% to $5K, 2.4% to $50K, 1.9% to $200K | Fee can be split ([DomainsDealer](https://domainsdealer.com/blog/how-much-does-domain-escrow-cost)) |

On a $5,000 sale, net proceeds run from about **$3,500 to $4,500** depending on venue and settings. Commission is therefore a major lever in the break-even formula. The practical setup is to list every name on Afternic with Fast Transfer at a **fixed buy-it-now price with LTO enabled**, and to cross-list on Sedo or Spaceship. Afternic says a buy-it-now price raises the chance of selling "by up to 65%" (self-reported) ([Afternic](https://www.afternic.com/sell-domains)). Keep make-offer for the rare name whose comps you cannot pin down. For most investor .coms, price between **$1,500 and $5,000**.

## Source from expiring names and comps, and value with NameBio

**Sourcing.** Hand registration costs only the fee, but it needs about **1% STR at $2–3K prices** to work ([NamePros](https://www.namepros.com/blog/domain-name-sell-through-rates.1164616/)). Most of what is still available to hand-register is unsellable. Expiring domains are the better hunting ground for a beginner. GoDaddy **retired its backorder service on October 7, 2025** but still runs expiry auctions (about $5 a year membership). **DropCatch** charges about $59 per backorder, refunded on failure, and contested catches go to auction. **NameJet and SnapNames** (about $69–79) share one pool, so do not backorder the same name on both ([domaindetails](https://domaindetails.com/kb/domain-investing/expired-domain-auctions-comparison)). **Park.io** (about $99 on success) covers hacker TLDs like .io ([names.center](https://names.center/how-to-buy-an-expired-domain.html)). The research found no sourced 2026 data on wholesale market price levels on NamePros or on success rates for private outreach to owners, so expect to calibrate these channels yourself.

**Valuation.** Use **NameBio comparable sales** as your anchor, the way you would use closed comps for a property. Automated appraisals rank names reasonably but miss individual prices badly. A NamePros test found GoDaddy's GoValue **overestimated by about 2.4x**, with only a 0.50 correlation to actual prices ([NamePros](https://www.namepros.com/threads/how-accurate-is-godaddy-appraisal-govalue.1065693/)). A small test found it **undervalued brandables by an average of 58%** ([Pearl Street Ventures](https://pearlstreetventures.com/blog/godaddy-appraisal-vs-estibot-accuracy)). GoDaddy is rebuilding its appraisal on an LLM stack ([GoDaddy](https://www.godaddy.com/resources/news/godaddys-domain-value-appraisal)), but no independent accuracy data exists for the new version. Ignore the widely repeated "75–85% accuracy" claim, which has no traceable study behind it. For each candidate, ask four questions. Are there comps for similar names? How many other extensions of the keyword are already registered (a proxy for end-user demand)? How many operating businesses use the term? Who is advertising on it? Your real estate background is an edge here. You know which property-tech, lending, home-services and local-market terms real operators pay for, and judging buyer demand is the hardest part of domain valuation.

**Outbound selling** is a high-volume, low-yield channel. The 2026 average cold-email reply rate is **3.43%**, and conversion to a deal is about **0.2%, or roughly one per 464–500 emails** ([Autobound](https://www.autobound.ai/blog/cold-email-guide-2026); [Reachoutly](https://reachoutly.com/cold-email/conversion-rate/)). No domain-specific benchmark exists. Outbound works only with a tight list, such as companies already using the term on another extension. Send from a secondary domain with SPF, DKIM and DMARC set up ([Unify](https://www.unifygtm.com/explore/cold-email-2026-domain-setup-deliverability-sequences)).

## Legal, registry and tax risks are manageable with three habits

**Trademark law is the one risk that can cost more than the domain itself.** WIPO handled a record **6,282 UDRP cases in 2025**, the main arbitration process for disputed domains ([WIPO](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/amc/w/news-domain-name-disputes/2026/news_0001); [DNW](https://domainnamewire.com/2026/01/15/wipo-handles-record-number-of-domain-disputes-in-2025/)). Only about 5% of complaints were denied. Over a recent 30-day window, **94% of decisions ordered a transfer** ([Robbie's Blog](https://www.robbiesblog.com/wipo-udrp-cases-2026-10-21/)). A complainant must show three things: the domain is confusingly similar to its mark, you have no legitimate interest in it, and you registered and used it in bad faith. The UDRP's only remedy is transfer. The bigger danger is the US **Anti-Cybersquatting Consumer Protection Act (ACPA)**, which allows **$1,000–$100,000 in statutory damages per domain** ([Harvard Berkman](https://cyber.harvard.edu/property00/domain/legislation.html)). Facebook won $2.795M against the operators of 105 domains ([ICA](https://www.internetcommerce.org/blog/cadna_acpa/)). Generic terms have real defenses. The USPTO **refused OpenAI's "GPT" trademark as descriptive and possibly generic** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/02/15/no-gpt-trademark-for-openai)), and a UDRP over creditgpt.com failed ([ICA](https://www.internetcommerce.org/?p=8099)). Names built on a specific AI brand, such as ChatGPT, OpenAI, Midjourney, Perplexity, or "Claude" or "Gemini" in an AI context, invite both a UDRP loss and ACPA exposure. **Habit one:** before every purchase, search the USPTO database (especially classes 9 and 42 for software) and WIPO's Global Brand Database. Never park a name on a page that shows ads for a trademark owner's competitors.

**Registry risk is real for .ai and .io and small for .com.** .ai fees made up **47.5% of Anguilla's government revenue in January–July 2026** (EC$217M, about US$80M) ([Jamaica Gleaner](https://jamaica-gleaner.com/index%2Ephp/article/business/20261009/anguillas-ai-domain-registration-earns-us80m-year-date-more-all-taxes)). That gives the government a strong fiscal reason to raise prices again. Identity Digital's five-year registry deal, signed in October 2024, points to a renegotiation around 2029 ([DNW](https://domainnamewire.com/2024/10/15/identity-digital-inks-ai-domain-deal-with-anguilla/)). For .io, the UK–Mauritius Chagos treaty (signed May 2025) is **stalled**. The US withheld support, and the implementing bill lapsed when the parliamentary session ended on April 29, 2026 ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/2/uk-mauritius-chagos-sovereignty-deal-stalls-amid-fears-of-further-delays); [Domain Incite](https://domainincite.com/31642-io-safe-for-now-as-trump-puts-chagos-deal-on-ice)). Even if the treaty is ratified, retirement only starts if "IO" is removed from ISO 3166-1, followed by a phase-out of **five to ten years** ([EuroDNS](https://www.eurodns.com/blog/end-of-io-domain)). The near-term technical risk is low. The real cost is lost liquidity, because buyers discount the uncertainty. Country-code extensions answer to national governments, not ICANN, and that has gone wrong before. Libya seized vb.ly in 2010 ([TheDomains](https://thedomains.com/2010/10/08/days-after-vb-ly-was-seized-by-the-libyan-registry-they-issue-a-statement-warning-on-any-4-letter-domain)), and Montenegro moved to nationalize .me in August 2026 ([Domain Incite](https://domainincite.com/31858-government-moves-to-nationalize-me)).

**Account security and escrow are routine but essential.** Domain theft usually happens through registrar-account takeover. **Habit two:** use a unique email address, hardware-key or app-based 2FA (not SMS), and a transfer lock on every name. Close every off-platform sale through Escrow.com, and type the escrow URL yourself, because fake escrow sites are the top scam. Never transfer a name before escrow confirms funds ([Namefi](https://namefi.io/r/en/glossary/domain-hijacking); [DomainInvesting](https://domaininvesting.com/hijacking-domain-names-for/)).

**Taxes need a CPA conversation before your first sale.** **Habit three:** keep a ledger of cost basis, acquisition date, renewals and sale proceeds for every name from day one. Domains held for investment are generally capital assets, taxed at long-term capital gains rates after a year. If you buy and sell regularly, the IRS may treat you as a **dealer**, which means ordinary income and possibly self-employment tax ([names.center](https://names.center/domain-name-capital-gains-tax-calculator.html)). Domains bought for use in a trade or business are generally amortized over **15 years under §197** ([IRS CCA 201543014](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-wd/201543014.pdf); [RSM](https://rsmus.com/insights/tax-alerts/archives/irs-provides-guidance-on-treatment-of-costs-to-acquire-domain-na.html)). No authoritative guidance says whether a pure investor should deduct or capitalize renewal fees. Marketplaces issue Form 1099-K only above **$20,000 and 200 transactions** ([CPA Practice Advisor](https://cpapracticeadvisor.com/2025/07/30/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-changes-1099-thresholds/165863/)), but all income is taxable whether or not a form is issued. Since you already run a real estate business, ask your CPA whether domain activity belongs in a separate LLC. An LLC mainly separates liability, which matters given ACPA exposure. It does not by itself change how gains are taxed.

## A 90-day plan with three budget tiers

These budget tiers come from the convergent beginner guidance: start with $100–500 to learn, hold 5–20 names rather than 50, and reserve three or more years of renewals ([Name.com](https://www.name.com/blog/how-to-start-domain-investing); [nectarnames](https://www.nectarnames.com/guides/getting-started-domain-investing); [wpdomainforsale](https://wpdomainforsale.com/domain-flipping-for-beginners/)). The specific allocations are the research team's own illustration, not a named expert's plan.

## Beginners fail in predictable ways, and AI makes one failure easier

The recurring mistakes are well documented: **trademark-infringing names**, bulk **hand-registered junk**, bets on **new gTLDs**, **overpaying at auction**, **too many names**, and **forgetting that renewals compound**. Guides sum it up as "five strong domains beat fifty weak ones" ([domaindetails](https://domaindetails.com/kb/domain-investing/common-domain-buying-mistakes)). Operational slips such as missed renewals and poor tracking also cost real money ([Domavest](https://www.domavest.com/2026/04/operational-mistakes-that-cost.html)). Good names can take **three to five years** to find a buyer. Survivorship bias distorts the advice you will hear: the people posting wins are the ones who stayed ([DNW](https://domainnamewire.com/2021/08/09/survivorship-bias-in-the-domain-business/); [Domavest](https://www.domavest.com/2026/03/how-to-evaluate-advice-from-domain.html)). Ignore "97% fail" statistics, which circulate without data.

**AI tools help most with the tedious work and hurt most at the creative end.** Elliot Silver says ChatGPT and Grok help him find unregistered names faster ([DomainInvesting.com](https://domaininvesting.com/?p=48909)), and guides pair LLMs with generators like Namelix for ideas ([domaindetails](https://domaindetails.com/kb/domain-investing/ai-domain-tools-valuation)). Better uses include filtering expiring-domain lists against your buying criteria, summarizing NameBio comps, flagging obvious trademark conflicts (as a first pass only, never a substitute for USPTO and WIPO searches), drafting outreach to likely end users, and building your renewal-ratio spreadsheet. The trap is that an LLM can produce a hundred plausible brandables in seconds, and buying them is the fastest way into the "too many junk names" mistake. Run every AI idea through the same comp and trademark filters as everything else.

**Communities and resources.** The core ecosystem is stable. **NamePros** is the forum, with deep sell-through analyses. **Domain Name Wire** offers news and a weekly podcast where investors share annual results. **DNJournal** publishes weekly sales charts and an annual State of the Industry survey. **NameBio** is the comps database. **DomainInvesting.com**, **DomainGang** and the **DomainSherpa** interview archive round out the reading list ([DNW podcast](https://podcasts.apple.com/ca/podcast/domain-name-wire-podcast/id934519368); [DNJournal SOTI 2026](https://www.dnjournal.com/cover/2026/soti/january.htm); [DomainSherpa](https://domainsherpa.com/tag/domain-name-sales/)). DNAcademy's course content went to GoDaddy in 2022, and copies sold elsewhere are pirated ([MarketScreener](https://www.marketscreener.com/quote/stock/GODADDY-INC-21452893/news/GoDaddy-Inc-acquired-Content-and-Related-Assets-of-DNAcademy-from-Web-X-0-Media-LLC-39869727/)).

**Realistic return expectations.** No independent study measures domainer profitability. Practitioner consensus is that investing is "not fast, easy or sure", and that many investors take years to turn a profit ([NamePros](https://www.namepros.com/blog/domain-name-investing-is-not-fast-easy-or-sure.1331096/)). A reasonable expectation: **year one is net negative**, with zero to two sales. **Years two and three approach break-even** if you prune hard. A minority who learn buying and pricing are profitable by year three. A vendor estimate puts disciplined, curated premium portfolios at **10–25% blended annual returns** over several years, and many low-STR portfolios at under 10% or a net loss ([Pixelworks](https://pixelworksdomains.com/blog/whats-a-realistic-annual-return-on-a-premium-domain-portfolio)). Treat that as an upper bound, not a promise. Returns are lumpy. Even Allemann's STR fell by almost a third from one year to the next.

## Conclusion

The AI era has stretched the domain market apart. The top end is richer than ever, and the speculative middle is crowded with machine-generated names chasing buyers who now name themselves. For a beginner, the advantage does not lie in finding the next AI keyword. It lies in discipline the crowd lacks: buying only names you can justify with comps and the break-even formula, pricing them at real retail, and pruning without sentiment. A real estate investor already knows this game: carrying costs, absorption rates and comps decide outcomes long before the exit. The same is true here.

The most useful near-term moves are cheap and concrete. Lock in .com renewals on names you will keep before November 1. Spend the first month reading comps instead of buying. Treat every .ai purchase as a two-year, $160-plus commitment that needs top-tier conviction. Domains look less like a get-rich play than like a small, slow-turning inventory business. That suits patient investors who can absorb a few years of tuition, and it punishes anyone who reads $70M headlines as their own odds. Before committing real capital, re-verify the fee schedules and headline figures cited here on their primary pages.
