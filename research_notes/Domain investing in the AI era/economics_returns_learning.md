## 1. Sell-through rates (STR): typical levels, by TLD and price point

### Takeaway
For passive, priced-to-retail portfolios, a 1–2% annual sell-through rate is the most often cited "solid" benchmark. Industry-wide rates across everything registered are well under 1%, and only tightly curated portfolios claim 3–5%. The best hard data point is Andrew Allemann (Domain Name Wire): 1.96% in 2023 and an annualized 1.36% in H1 2024. Doron Vermaat (Efty) reported 38 sales in 2025 at a $9,436 average and a $4,995 median.

### Cited Findings
- STR definition: the percentage of a portfolio's names that sell in one calendar year — [Namecheap blog](https://namecheap.com/blog/domain-name-sell-through-rate); [NameSilo](https://www.namesilo.com/blog/en/domain-name-search/domain-portfolio-sell-through-rate-str-explained)
- Andrew Allemann (DNW) H1 2024: 17 sales vs 23 in H1 2023. Annualized STR was 1.36%, down from 1.96%. He said that "for most passive portfolios like mine, a sell-through rate of 1-2% is considered solid." — [Domain Name Wire, "My no good first half of 2024"](https://domainnamewire.com/2024/07/02/my-no-good-first-half-of-2024/); also cited in [NamePros, "Is the domain name market up or down?"](https://www.namepros.com/blog/is-the-domain-name-market-up-or-down.1334181/)
- Allemann released full-year 2023, 2024 and 2025 results as podcasts covering sales count, median price, TLD mix and hold times. The search snippets did not include the full-year figures. — [DNW #470 (2023)](https://domainnamewire.com/2024/01/22/my-2023-domain-sales-results-dnw-podcast-470/); [DNW #512 (2024)](https://domainnamewire.com/2024/12/09/my-2024-domain-sales-results-dnw-podcast-512/); [DNW #570 (2025, Jan 19 2026)](https://domainnamewire.com/2026/01/19/my-2025-domain-sales-results-dnw-podcast-570/)
- Doron Vermaat (Efty co-founder, brandable investor) 2025: 38 domains sold for $358,563. That was fewer sales than prior years, but the average sale price rose to $9,436 and the median was $4,995. — [DomainBlog, Jan 2 2026](https://www.domainblog.com/2026/01/02/doron-from-efty-share-his-2025-numbers/). The portfolio size, and so the STR, was not in the snippet.
- Earlier DomainSherpa profile of Vermaat: "$40K annual profit from part-time brandable domain name investing" (older interview) — [DomainSherpa](https://domainsherpa.com/doron-vermaat-namerockstar/)
- "Most sellers quote personal sell-through-rates of 1–3%" — [NamePros, Domain Name Sell-Through Rates (Bob Hawkes)](https://www.namepros.com/blog/domain-name-sell-through-rates.1164616/)
- Bob Hawkes' 2019-era estimate of apparent industry-wide STR: .com 0.601%, all TLDs 0.476%. Commenters disputed it as distorted by registrar and auction sales. — [NamePros](https://www.namepros.com/blog/domain-name-sell-through-rates.1164616/). Hawkes later wrote that industry-wide .com STR is "almost certainly less than 1%", but higher for a premium name bought at around $500. — [NamePros, The Minimum Domain Price for Profitability](https://www.namepros.com/blog/the-minimum-domain-price-for-profitability.1313195/)
- In "Eleven Things About Domain Name Sell-Through Rates" (Sept 2024), Hawkes argues that STR is calculated after the fact and its main use is deciding whether to keep holding a name. He notes that the higher annual costs of .ai, .io and some new extensions must be justified by higher STR or prices. — [NamePros](https://www.namepros.com/blog/eleven-things-about-domain-name-sell-through-rates.1334806/)
- Elliot Silver discussed how to calculate STR when a portfolio is still growing, since the denominator keeps changing — [DomainInvesting.com](https://domaininvesting.com/how-to-calculate-str-when-youre-still-buying-domain-names). Details not retrieved.
- Michael Cyger (DNAcademy founder) listed STR, defined as sales per domain per year, as one of five priorities for his own portfolio in 2025. The others included portfolio pruning, portfolio hygiene and dollar volume per listing. He published no figures. — [NamePros](https://www.namepros.com/blog/michael-cyger-shares-domain-investing-priorities-for-2025.1342404/)
- Alternative metric: revenue per domain (RPD), argued to beat STR because it combines sale frequency and price — [mi.ke/rpd](https://mi.ke/rpd/). Atom's example: "if your RPD is $30 but your average acquisition price is $100, you'll only break even if you sell within three years." — [Atom blog](https://www.atom.com/blog/the-math-behind-domain-investing-how-to-use-data-to-maximize-returns/)
- Vendor and secondary claims: "1% to 2% annual sell-through rate is common; focused, well-curated portfolios can reach 3% to 5%" — [Pixelworks Domains (vendor)](https://pixelworksdomains.com/blog/sell-through-rate-the-metric-that-predicts-your-domain-profits). One secondary guide claims an industry STR of "3–4%" from 2024 data, which conflicts with the Hawkes analysis and is unverified. — [domaindetails.com](https://domaindetails.com/kb/domain-investing/domain-portfolio-analytics-roi)
- .ai market context: NameBio-tracked .ai dollar volume rose 107% in 2024 — [Wikipedia: Domain aftermarket (citing NameBio)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domain_aftermarket). In Oct 2026, one seller's batch of 13 .ai sales totaled about $1.4M, led by Act.ai at $375K and Pad.ai and Critical.ai at $150K each — [DomainGang](https://domaingang.com/domain-news/ai-aftermarket-surges-with-1-4-million-in-fresh-domain-sales/). A late-Oct 2025 DNJournal chart was led by a "record-breaking .AI sale" — [DNJournal](https://www.dnjournal.com/archive/domainsales/2025/1105.htm)
- .ai average sale price figures conflict sharply: about $6,525 (Afternic 2024 data) versus a claimed $239,516 for 2026 with unclear methodology — [domaindetails.com .AI guide](https://domaindetails.com/kb/domain-investing/ai-domain-investing-guide). The higher figure should be treated as unreliable.
- New gTLDs: guides say the .net and new gTLD aftermarket is "thin" and that .store/.online/.site "rarely sell profitably" — [domaindetails.com](https://domaindetails.com/kb/domain-investing/common-domain-buying-mistakes) (vendor blog)

### Inferences
- A realistic beginner planning assumption is 0.5–1% STR in years 1–2, because new investors' names are weaker than veterans'. Plan on 1–2% only once names are curated and listed on Afternic/Sedo/Atom with BIN prices.
- STR alone misleads. A 3% STR at a $500 average sale price earns less than 1% at $5,000, so RPD or net revenue per name is the better metric.
- For .ai, the much higher renewal cost (see section 2) means the STR or price must be several times higher than for .com to justify holding. Recent headline .ai sales are top-of-market outcomes, not typical ones.

### Gaps
- I found no published sell-through figures from Andrew Rosener, Mike Mann (DomainMarket), Shane Cultra, Joe Uddeme or Ammar Kubba. Searches did not return their portfolio-level STR. Uddeme reports aggregate brokerage figures (see section 4) rather than personal STR.
- Allemann's exact full-year 2024 and 2025 STR and median price figures sit in podcast pages I could not open.
- I found no independent, dataset-based STR comparison of .com vs .ai vs new gTLDs by price band.

## 2. Holding economics: renewals, break-even math and the .com price increase

### Takeaway
At about $11–12 per year per .com, renewals are the main drag. With a 1,000-name portfolio, a $2,000 average sale price and 20% commission, break-even needs only about 0.6–0.7% STR before acquisition costs. Verisign raises the .com wholesale price from $10.26 to $10.97 on Nov 1, 2026 (+6.9%). .ai renewals of roughly $80–160 per year change the math entirely.

### Cited Findings
- Verisign .com wholesale rises from $10.26 to $10.97 per domain-year, effective Nov 1, 2026 (disclosed in the Q1 2026 earnings call). With the $0.20 ICANN fee, the registrar floor goes from about $10.46 to $11.17. — [Motley Fool Q1 2026 transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/04/23/verisign-vrsn-q1-2026-earnings-transcript/); [Domain Name Wire, Nov 19 2025](https://domainnamewire.com/2025/11/19/verisign-can-increase-com-prices-in-2026/); [Openprovider](https://www.openprovider.com/blog/com-price-increase-domain-resellers)
- History: 7% .com increases in each of 2021, 2022, 2023 and 2024, with none in 2025. Names renewed before Nov 1, 2026 lock the current price for the full term bought, up to 10 years. — [Openprovider](https://www.openprovider.com/blog/are-domain-prices-going-up); [tldes.com](https://tldes.com/pages/com-price-increase-2026)
- .net wholesale rises from $10.91 to $12.00, effective Mar 1, 2027 (announced Aug 27, 2026) — same search set ([Openprovider](https://www.openprovider.com/blog/are-domain-prices-going-up)). Treat this as needing verification.
- Break-even example: 1,000 names, $2,000 average sale price and 20% commission need 6.25 sales a year to cover renewals, so the required STR is about 0.6% — [Domavest (blog), Feb 2026](https://www.domavest.com/2026/02/the-real-economics-behind-buying-and.html)
- NamePros sets a "renewal ratio" test, (net sale value × STR) / annual renewal, and says it should be at least 1.0 to justify renewing — [NamePros, How I Ranked Every Name in a 2000+ Portfolio](https://www.namepros.com/blog/how-i-ranked-every-name-in-a-2000-domain-name-portfolio.1383894/)
- Example used in beginner guides: 100 domains at $12 per year cost $1,200 in annual renewals before any sale — [domaindetails.com](https://domaindetails.com/kb/domain-investing/common-domain-buying-mistakes)
- .ai renewal prices (Oct 2026): Dynadot about $85.60 per year; another registrar $98.99 per year with a two-year minimum; Spaceship $159.96 for two years — [domaindetails.com .AI guide](https://domaindetails.com/kb/domain-investing/ai-domain-investing-guide); [register.domains](https://register.domains/en/blog/should-i-buy-ai-domain-guide); [aitoolsbakery](https://aitoolsbakery.com/blog/ai-domain-price/). Premium .ai names can carry premium renewals.
- Marketplace commissions: Afternic charges 25% (Basic) or 30% (Boost), dropping to 15–20% with GoDaddy aftermarket nameservers. Lease-to-own commission discounts depend on term length. — [Afternic sell page](https://www.afternic.com/sell-domains); [wpdomainforsale fee comparison](https://wpdomainforsale.com/domain-marketplace-fees/)

### Inferences (own calculations from the figures above)
- Break-even formula: Required STR = renewal per name / (average sale price × (1 − commission)).
  - .com at $11.50 renewal, $2,000 ASP, 20% commission: 11.50 / 1,600 = 0.72% STR.
  - .com at $11.50, $1,000 ASP, 25% commission: 11.50 / 750 = 1.53% STR. That is above what most passive portfolios achieve, so low-priced, low-quality inventory tends to lose money.
  - .ai at $85 renewal, $2,000 ASP, 20% commission: 85 / 1,600 = 5.3% STR. At a $5,000 ASP it is still 2.1%, so .ai only works for names that are top-tier relative to the investor's buying skill.
- Portfolio example: 200 .com at $11.50 cost $2,300 per year. At 1% STR (2 sales) and $2,000 ASP with 20% commission, net is $3,200, a profit of about $900 before acquisition costs. At 0.5% STR (1 sale), net is $1,600, a $700 loss.
- Effect of the Nov 2026 increase: about +$0.71 per name per year. That adds roughly $710 a year on a 1,000-name portfolio and raises required STR by about 0.04 percentage points at a $2,000 ASP. It matters at the margin for hand-regs priced under $1,000 and is minor for curated names.
- Acquisition cost dominates early: names bought at auction for $200–$1,000 need years of sales to recover. Renewal math alone understates beginners' losses.

### Gaps
- Exact retail .com renewal prices after Nov 2026 at major registrars (Cloudflare at-cost, Porkbun, Namecheap, GoDaddy) were not retrieved.
- The .net 2027 increase came from a single search summary and needs confirmation on Verisign's site.

## 3. Pricing strategy: BIN vs make-offer vs lease-to-own, and retail price points

### Takeaway
Marketplace data and practitioners favor fixed buy-it-now (BIN) prices for the "fast lane" sales networks. Lease-to-own (LTO) raises average prices: Afternic reports LTO deals had a 35% higher average sale price than BIN in H1 2024. Make-offer is usually kept for premium or hard-to-price names. Most investor sales cluster in the low-to-mid four figures, for example Vermaat's $4,995 median.

### Cited Findings
- Afternic: from January to June 2024, LTO transactions had a 35% higher average sale price than BIN, based on GoDaddy data for names priced $495–$49,999 — [Afternic blog](https://blog.afternic.com/lease-to-own-asp/)
- Afternic claims a BIN price can raise the chance of selling "by up to 65%", and that LTO can boost STR through monthly pricing (platform self-reported) — [Afternic sell page](https://www.afternic.com/sell-domains); [Afternic LTO guide](https://blog.afternic.com/lease-to-own-guide/)
- Afternic expanded LTO to self-brokerage and custom checkout links, and raised the LTO price ceiling (older guidance capped it at $100K) — [Afternic blog](https://blog.afternic.com/lease-to-own-self-brokerage/)
- Third-party guidance: BIN for names under about $5,000, make-offer for premium names. The source is unsourced and vendor-written. — [wpdomainforsale](https://wpdomainforsale.com/sedo-vs-afternic-vs-dan-com/)
- Price-point evidence: Vermaat's 2025 median of $4,995 against an average of $9,436 shows a few large sales skew the average — [DomainBlog](https://www.domainblog.com/2026/01/02/doron-from-efty-share-his-2025-numbers/)
- Cyger has given a tip on "pricing your domains to increase sell-through rates" — [DNW Podcast #386](https://domainnamewire.com/2022/05/02/domain-investing-education-dnw-podcast-386/). Specifics not retrieved.
- Bob Hawkes on minimum prices for profitability, given low STR — [NamePros](https://www.namepros.com/blog/the-minimum-domain-price-for-profitability.1313195/)

### Inferences
- With STR around 1%, price and STR trade off directly. Pricing at $1,500–$5,000 BIN with an LTO option is the common middle ground for .com investor inventory. Five-figure-plus prices suit only names with clear end-user comps on NameBio.
- Because a single sale can carry the year, many investors price boldly and accept lower STR. A beginner portfolio of weaker names cannot carry high prices.

### Gaps
- I found no independent data comparing conversion rates of BIN, make-offer and LTO at specific price points such as $2,500.

## 4. Realistic returns for beginners, survivorship bias and public results

### Takeaway
No independent study measures domainer profitability. Practitioner consensus is that most beginners lose money or break even in years 1–3, and that reported success stories reflect survivorship bias. The overall market is healthy and growing: NameBio recorded about $244M across about 190K sales in 2025, up 31.9% on 2024. Individual results are lumpy and slow.

### Cited Findings
- NameBio 2025: about 190,300 reported sales totaling more than $244M, up 31.9% in dollar volume from 2024 (2024: about 144,700 sales and $185M) — [NamePros analysis of NameBio data](https://www.namepros.com/blog/2025-domain-name-market-dollar-volume-trends-based-on-namebio-data.1374269/); [Wikipedia: Domain aftermarket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domain_aftermarket); also cited by Joe Uddeme's NameExperts ($244M, +31.9%) — [Name Experts 2026 trends](https://nameexperts.com/blog/domain-market-trends/)
- H1 2025: 93,100 NameBio sales, just over $122M, with strength in legacy TLDs, .ai, .io, .xyz and .co — [NamePros](https://www.namepros.com/threads/domain-name-sales-reported-at-namebio-during-first-six-months-of-2025-strength-for-legacy-extensions-ai.1356803/)
- Joe Uddeme: more than $125M in brokered sales since founding NameExperts in 2015 — [DNJournal SOTI 2025](https://www.dnjournal.com/cover/2025/january.htm). DNJournal's annual State of the Industry surveys 29–30 experts — [SOTI 2026](https://www.dnjournal.com/cover/2026/soti/january.htm)
- Survivorship bias: Domain Name Wire warns that looking only at people who "made it" and copying them is survivorship bias — [DNW, Aug 2021](https://domainnamewire.com/2021/08/09/survivorship-bias-in-the-domain-business/)
- NamePros: domain investing is "not fast, easy or sure". It took many domainers years to see a profit, and full-time investing should only be considered after several years of profitable sales. — [NamePros](https://www.namepros.com/blog/domain-name-investing-is-not-fast-easy-or-sure.1331096/)
- Vendor estimate: a disciplined, curated premium portfolio earns a 10–25% blended annual return over several years, while low STR with high costs often yields under 10% or a net loss — [Pixelworks (vendor)](https://pixelworksdomains.com/blog/whats-a-realistic-annual-return-on-a-premium-domain-portfolio)
- Allemann's 2024 dip (STR 1.96% to 1.36%) shows that even experienced passive investors see large year-to-year swings — [DNW](https://domainnamewire.com/2024/07/02/my-no-good-first-half-of-2024/)
- Unverified claims of "97% fail" and "most quit within 12 months" circulate on blogs without data — [The Website Flip](https://thewebsiteflip.com/domains/how-to-fail-domaining/); [Domavest](https://www.domavest.com/2026/02/the-truth-about-easy-money-in-domain.html). Do not cite these as fact.

### Inferences
- Realistic beginner outlook: year 1 is net negative (acquisition plus renewals, 0–2 sales). Years 2–3 bring possible first sales and around break-even if the investor has pruned aggressively. A minority who learn pricing and buying are profitable by year 3.
- Rising market volume (+31.9%) is driven heavily by premium and AI-related names. It does not mean low-end hand-regs are selling more.

### Gaps
- I found no independent survey of beginner profitability.
- DNAcademy case studies and DomainSherpa beginner outcomes were not retrievable. DNAcademy content was acquired by GoDaddy in March 2022 ([MarketScreener](https://www.marketscreener.com/quote/stock/GODADDY-INC-21452893/news/GoDaddy-Inc-acquired-Content-and-Related-Assets-of-DNAcademy-from-Web-X-0-Media-LLC-39869727/)), and its current availability is unclear.

## 5. Common beginner mistakes and starter budget plans

### Takeaway
Recurring mistakes: trademark-infringing names (UDRP or legal risk), hand-registering speculative junk, betting on new gTLDs, overpaying at auction, buying too many names, and not budgeting for renewals. Starter advice from guides converges on 5–20 carefully chosen names, a few hundred dollars, and a reserve for 2–3 years of renewals.

### Cited Findings
- Trademark terms lead to legal disputes and UDRP losses. A vendor blog claims complainants win 95%+ of UDRPs (unverified). — [domaindetails.com](https://domaindetails.com/kb/domain-investing/common-domain-buying-mistakes); [ICDSoft on cybersquatting](https://www.icdsoft.com/blog/domain-investing-vs-cybersquatting-key-differences-explained/)
- "Five strong domains beat fifty weak ones." Avoid speculative idea registrations without market evidence. — [domaindetails.com](https://domaindetails.com/kb/domain-investing/common-domain-buying-mistakes); [Domain Investing for Beginners](https://domainglossary.com/blog/domain-investing-for-beginners)
- Stick with .com unless there is a specific reason. The new gTLD market is thin. — [domaindetails.com](https://domaindetails.com/kb/domain-investing/common-domain-buying-mistakes)
- Operational mistakes (missed renewals, poor tracking) cost domainers money — [Domavest, Apr 2026](https://www.domavest.com/2026/04/operational-mistakes-that-cost.html)
- Good names can take 3–5 years to find a buyer — [domaindetails.com](https://domaindetails.com/kb/domain-investing/common-domain-buying-mistakes)
- Starter budgets: $100–500 "to learn without risking real money" — [Name.com](https://www.name.com/blog/how-to-start-domain-investing); [Openprovider guide](https://www.openprovider.com/blog/domain-investing). Start with 10–20 total, or 5–10 carefully selected names, not 50 — [nectarnames](https://www.nectarnames.com/guides/getting-started-domain-investing); [domaindetails strategy](https://domaindetails.com/kb/domain-investing/choosing-domain-investment-strategy). Budget for 3+ years of renewals before expecting sales — [wpdomainforsale](https://wpdomainforsale.com/domain-flipping-for-beginners/)
- Cyger's 2025 plan emphasizes pruning and portfolio hygiene, which counters the "too many names" mistake — [NamePros](https://www.namepros.com/blog/michael-cyger-shares-domain-investing-priorities-for-2025.1342404/)

### Inferences (illustrative budget plans built from the sourced guidance and section 2 math; these are not quoted from a named expert)
- $500: about 10–15 hand-registered or closeout .coms (around $12 each), with about $300 held back for learning tools and 2 years of renewals. The goal is learning, not profit. Use NameBio comps for every registration, and drop names that draw no inquiries after a year.
- $2,500: about 15–30 .coms, mixing a few $100–$300 expired-auction or closeout names with researched hand-regs. Keep about 30% in reserve for renewals (roughly $350 per year for 30 names after Nov 2026). List with BIN and LTO on Afternic and other platforms. One $2,000 sale at 20% commission covers about 4 years of renewals.
- $10,000: 30–60 names, or fewer, higher-quality .coms with end-user comps. Keep total renewals under about 10% of expected annual net sales. Consider a small .ai allocation only for names that clearly justify $85–160 per year renewals.

### Gaps
- I found no published $500/$2,500/$10,000 plans attributed to named veterans (Rosener, Mann, Silver, Cyger, Kupka).

## 7. How AI tools help the domain investor

### Takeaway
Investors use LLMs (ChatGPT, Claude, Grok) mainly for ideation and finding unregistered names, with generators like Namelix for brandables. Valuation still relies on NameBio comps, with automated appraisals (Estibot, GoDaddy) only as a cross-check. AI-sector demand is itself driving the market, especially .ai.

### Cited Findings
- Elliot Silver noted that AI tools like ChatGPT and Grok helped him find unregistered names faster. He also argued AI is fueling domain sales but "the cycle matters". — [DomainInvesting.com (2025)](https://domaininvesting.com/?p=48909)
- Guides pair ChatGPT for creative ideation with Namelix-style generators. They advise using 2–3 valuation tools and cross-referencing them. — [domaindetails.com AI tools](https://domaindetails.com/kb/domain-investing/ai-domain-tools-valuation)
- Treat automated appraisals (Estibot) as a cross-check against NameBio comps, not as prices — [Bluehost](https://www.bluehost.com/blog/domain-investing-strategies/)
- AI-related names drove headline 2025–26 sales (.ai records, $1.4M .ai batch) — [DomainGang](https://domaingang.com/domain-news/ai-aftermarket-surges-with-1-4-million-in-fresh-domain-sales/); [DNJournal](https://www.dnjournal.com/archive/domainsales/2025/1105.htm)

### Inferences
- High-value uses: (1) scripting NameBio, expired-list and drop-list filtering, (2) LLM screening of drop lists against buyer-intent criteria and trademark red flags, though LLM trademark checks are not a substitute for USPTO/WIPO searches, (3) drafting outreach to end users, while noting that mass unsolicited outreach is viewed negatively and risks spam or bad-faith perceptions, (4) building renewal-vs-keep spreadsheets using the renewal-ratio test, (5) summarizing comps.
- Risk: AI makes hand-registering plausible-sounding brandables trivially easy, which floods the market with low-quality inventory. LLM ideation increases the "too many junk names" mistake unless it is paired with strict comp-based filters.

### Gaps
- I found no data on how many investors use AI tools or on measurable ROI improvement from them.
