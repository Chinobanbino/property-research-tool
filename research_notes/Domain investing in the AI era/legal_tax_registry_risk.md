# Legal, Tax, and Registry Risks for Domain Name Investors (as of October 2026)

*Scope: US investors, with international notes where relevant. Research date: 2026-10-21. These are research notes, not legal or tax advice. Trademark, ACPA, and tax decisions should go to a trademark attorney and a CPA or tax attorney.*
*Method note: direct fetches of wipo.int, domainnamewire.com and webhosting.today failed with DNS errors in this environment. Figures from those sites come from search-result summaries of the pages, not from full-page reads. Where a figure is secondhand, the notes say so.*

## UDRP, ACPA, URS, and trademark screening (including AI brand names)

### Takeaway
UDRP filings at WIPO reached a record of about 6,282 cases in 2025, only slightly above 2023 and 2024. Complainants win the large majority of decided cases: about 5% are denied, about 15% settle, and in recent tracking about 94% of decisions ordered a transfer. Reverse Domain Name Hijacking (RDNH) findings are also at a record (86 in 2025), but they carry no monetary penalty under the UDRP. The real money risk is the US ACPA, which allows statutory damages of $1,000–$100,000 per domain. For AI names, "GPT" has been refused registration by the USPTO as descriptive or generic, so generic-term .ai and "GPT" names have defenses. Exact or near-exact brand terms (OpenAI, ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) remain high-risk.

### Cited Findings
**WIPO caseload**
- WIPO announced that 2025 was a record year, with more than 6,200 domain cases, its highest caseload ever. WIPO has resolved over 80,000 cases in 25 years: over 70,000 gTLD cases and nearly 10,000 ccTLD cases. Most complainants came from the US, France, and the UK — [WIPO press release, Jan 2026](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/amc/w/news-domain-name-disputes/2026/news_0001)
- The exact 2025 figure is 6,282, against 6,192 in 2023 and 6,168 in 2024, so growth was marginal. At WIPO, about 5% of disputes were denied and about 15% settled before a decision. The rest ended in transfer or cancellation — [Domain Name Wire, 2026-01-15](https://domainnamewire.com/2026/01/15/wipo-handles-record-number-of-domain-disputes-in-2025/) (secondhand via search summary)
- WIPO also highlighted a 2025 panel decision that set heightened scrutiny for AI-generated pleadings — [WIPO press release](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/amc/w/news-domain-name-disputes/2026/news_0001)
- WIPO's Overview of panel views (the "WIPO Jurisprudential Overview") is the main reference for how panels decide cases. Commentary says a February 2026 update ("3.1") addresses AI-assisted arguments and evidence for the first time — [WIPO Overview](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/amc/domain-name-disputes/overview/index); [igerent.com commentary](https://igerent.com/ai-domain-disputes-wipo-overview-3-1-post)
- Since 9 March 2026, WIPO has offered an optional "UDRP Priority Service" for faster processing. It does not change the substantive criteria — per search summary of [Dreyfus guide](https://www.dreyfus.fr/en/2025/08/06/complete-guide-2025-domain-name-disputes-udrp-procedure-syreli-and-international-alternatives/)
- 2026 pace: in mid-October 2026, a third-party tracker logged about 15–27 new WIPO cases per day. In the 30 days to 21 Oct 2026, it counted 376 WIPO decisions, 94% of them transfers — [Robbie's Blog, 2026-10-21](https://www.robbiesblog.com/wipo-udrp-cases-2026-10-21/); [2026-10-20](https://www.robbiesblog.com/wipo-udrp-cases-2026-10-20/)
- How the UDRP works: the complainant must show (1) the domain is identical or confusingly similar to its mark, (2) the registrant has no rights or legitimate interests in it, and (3) the domain was registered and is being used in bad faith. The standard of proof is the balance of probabilities. The only remedies are transfer or cancellation, with no money damages — [DCC / GigaLaw comparison summaries](https://giga.law/blog/2015/07/06/when-to-file-a-urs-complaint-instead-of-or-in-addition-to-a-udrp-complaint); [CircleID](https://circleid.com/posts/20180423_what_is_uniform_rapid_suspension_system_urs_what_is_it_good_for/)

**RDNH (reverse domain name hijacking)**
- UDRP.tools counted 86 RDNH findings across WIPO and Forum in 2025, up from 56 in 2024, 50 in 2023 and 47 in 2022. Panels considered RDNH in more than 200 cases in 2025, up from 122 in 2024 — [Domain Name Wire, "2025 in review: RDNH", 2026-01-06](https://domainnamewire.com/2026/01/06/2025-in-review-reverse-domain-name-hijacking/) (secondhand)
- GigaLaw's quarterly digest put RDNH at 1.3% (20) of cases decided in Q2 2025 and 1.2% (26) in Q3 2025 — [GigaLaw UDRP Digest](https://giga.law/blog/2025/7/25/udrp-digest)
- In 2023, 49 disputes produced RDNH findings, close to one in five denials — per search summary of [Domain Name Wire](https://domainnamewire.com/?p=79226)

**ACPA (15 U.S.C. §1125(d))**
- Statutory damages range from $1,000 to $100,000 per domain name, or actual damages if those are higher. The ACPA requires "bad faith intent to profit" — [Harvard Berkman ACPA page](https://cyber.harvard.edu/property00/domain/legislation.html); [ICANNWiki](https://www.icannwiki.org/Anti-Cybersquatting_Consumer_Protection_Act)
- Example award: Facebook won $2.795M against operators of 105 domains, about $26.6K per domain (N.D. Cal., 2013). One commentator says courts have generally awarded amounts closer to the low end of the range — [Domain Name Wire / ICA commentary via search](https://www.internetcommerce.org/blog/cadna_acpa/)
- Registrants who lose a UDRP case can file an ACPA action in US court to block the transfer. Brand owners found to have reverse-hijacked have in some cases paid $100K+ — [revisionlegal.com](https://revisionlegal.com/cybersquatting-lawyer/what-is-cybersquatting/) (claim not quantified by frequency)

**URS (new gTLDs)**
- The Uniform Rapid Suspension (URS) applies to new gTLDs and a few legacy gTLDs that opted in. It does not apply to .com. The standard is "clear and convincing evidence" with no genuine issue of material fact. The only remedy is suspension for the rest of the registration term; there is no transfer. Filing costs about $375, and a determination takes about 18 days in a typical case — [Dreyfus, 2026-01-06](https://www.dreyfus.fr/en/2026/01/06/when-is-urs-a-strategic-option-for-trademark-owners/); [CircleID 2020](https://circleid.com/posts/20200827-uniform-rapid-suspension-not-appropriate-for-com-domain-names); [GigaLaw](https://giga.law/blog/2015/07/06/when-to-file-a-urs-complaint-instead-of-or-in-addition-to-a-udrp-complaint) (fee is from an older blog; verify the current fee with the provider)

**AI brand names and trademarks**
- In February 2024 the USPTO issued a final refusal of OpenAI's "GPT" application as merely descriptive of "generative pre-trained transformer". The examiner also warned it "appears to be generic". This was the second refusal; the first application was filed in December 2022. OpenAI could appeal to the TTAB — [TechCrunch, 2024-02-15](https://techcrunch.com/2024/02/15/no-gpt-trademark-for-openai); [Saul Ewing](https://www.saul.com/insights/alert/what-gpt-and-who-owns-it); [Goodmans](https://www.goodmans.ca/insights/post/goodmans-ip-blog/openai-s-gpt-trademark-application-rejected-by-uspto)
- creditgpt.com UDRP: the complaint, based on a descriptive AI mark on the Supplemental Register, was dismissed. The panel also held that renewing a domain, or transferring it to a company under the same ownership, is not a "new registration" — [ICA, vol. 6.27](https://www.internetcommerce.org/?p=8099)
- .ai UDRPs in 2026 (the "DAI" case series at WIPO) include respondents claiming registrar-auction purchases for AI projects, and GoDaddy AI-generated parking content being argued as not the registrant's choice. For example, an NCAA "NIL" .ai complaint was denied for lack of bad-faith registration — [WIPO DAI2026-0003](https://www.wipo.int/amc/en/domains/decisions/pdf/2026/dai2026-0003.pdf); [DAI2026-0001](https://www.wipo.int/amc/en/domains/decisions/pdf/2026/dai2026-0001.pdf); [ICA vol. 6.29](https://www.internetcommerce.org/udrp-case-summaries/when-ai-cuts-both-ways-applying-udrp-perspectives-on-targeting-vol-6-29/)
- A WIPO case involving AI chip company Tenstorrent was filed in October 2026 (D2026-4550) — [search summary of Robbie's Blog trackers](https://www.robbiesblog.com/wipo-udrp-cases-2026-10-14/)

### Inferences
- UDRP outcomes heavily favor complainants overall, but those statistics are dominated by clear-cut typosquats. Investors holding genuinely generic or descriptive names win more often, and RDNH is increasingly found. Still, RDNH has no financial penalty under the UDRP, so the deterrent is reputational only.
- Screening workflow to suggest (an inference from the above, not sourced as a standard): check the USPTO trademark search system for live marks in the relevant classes (especially 9 and 42 for AI software and SaaS), plus WIPO Global Brand Database for international marks. Avoid names that contain a distinctive brand (OpenAI, ChatGPT, Claude+AI context, Gemini+AI context, Copilot, Midjourney, Perplexity). Do not use parking pages that show ads for the brand owner's competitors. "GPT" as a generic suffix is defensible but not risk-free: OpenAI may still assert common-law rights or usage policies.
- "Claude" and "Gemini" are dictionary words or common names, so registering them alone is lower risk. Combinations with AI context (e.g., "claudeai", "geminichat") target the brand and are high risk under both the UDRP and the ACPA.

## US tax treatment of domain names

### Takeaway
Domains bought from a third party for a trade or business are generally capitalized and amortized over 15 years under §197 (IRS CCA 201543014). Domains held for investment are generally capital assets, giving long-term capital gains treatment after one year. Investors who buy and sell regularly risk dealer status, which means ordinary income, possible self-employment tax, and inventory treatment. The 1099-K threshold is back to more than $20,000 and more than 200 transactions (OBBBA, retroactive). Sales tax and VAT treatment of domain sales is unsettled and needs professional advice.

### Cited Findings
- IRS Chief Counsel Memo 201543014 (2015): costs to acquire a non-generic, trademark-type domain used in a business are §197 intangibles, amortized ratably over 15 years starting the month of acquisition. For generic domains, treatment depends on the facts, and a §197 analysis may still apply — [IRS CCA 201543014 (PDF)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-wd/201543014.pdf); [RSM tax alert](https://rsmus.com/insights/tax-alerts/archives/irs-provides-guidance-on-treatment-of-costs-to-acquire-domain-na.html); [Parker Tax](https://www.parkertaxpublishing.com/public/tax-domain-names-capitalized-amortized.html)
- The registration period is not treated as a useful life. A domain with no business use can be depreciated under §167 only if a useful life can be established — per [The Tax Adviser, May 2016](https://www.thetaxadviser.com/newsletters/2016/may/how-internet-domain-names-are-taxed/)
- Annual registration and renewal fees are commonly treated as deductible, but this comes from community or forum sources, not IRS guidance — [Intuit TurboTax community](https://ttlc.intuit.com/community/deductions-credits-9/website-purchases-are-assets-or-expenses-826911) (low-authority; verify)
- Investor vs dealer: a casual investor holding longer than a year generally gets long-term capital gains. Someone in the trade or business of buying and selling domains may be treated as a dealer, which means ordinary income and self-employment tax regardless of holding period — [names.center calculator explainer](https://names.center/domain-name-capital-gains-tax-calculator.html) (commercial source; this follows the general IRC §1221 "property held primarily for sale to customers" rule)
- 1099-K: OBBBA §70432 (signed 4 July 2025) restored IRC §6050W(e) to more than $20,000 in payments and more than 200 transactions, retroactive to post-2021 years. The $2,500/$600 phase-ins never took effect. Both tests must be met. All income is still taxable whether or not a form is issued — [CPA Practice Advisor, 2025-07-30](https://cpapracticeadvisor.com/2025/07/30/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-changes-1099-thresholds/165863/); [TaxAct](https://blog.taxact.com/4-common-misconceptions-about-the-new-1099-k-reporting-threshold/); [Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/us/en/blog/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-1099-reporting-threshold.html)
- 1099-NEC and 1099-MISC thresholds rise to $2,000 for tax year 2026 under OBBBA. This is relevant to commissions or brokers paid by an investor's business — [Patriot Software](https://www.patriotsoftware.com/blog/accounting/1099-reporting-threshold/)
- EU VAT: domain sellers on NamePros report that recent EU rules led some sellers to stop selling to EU business buyers via certain platforms or to route sales through Afternic. These reports are anecdotal and unverified — [NamePros thread](https://thenamepros.com/goto/post?id=8329147)

### Inferences
- A practical split many advisers use: track each domain's cost basis (purchase price plus capitalized acquisition costs) and holding period. Report sales on Form 8949/Schedule D as an investor, or on Schedule C (or an entity return) if the activity is a business. Renewals for an investor (not a business) may be capitalizable carrying costs rather than deductible. This is a key question for a CPA.
- An LLC (single-member, disregarded by default) mainly provides liability separation. It matters given ACPA exposure, but liability protection is limited if the member personally commits cybersquatting. It does not by itself change capital-vs-ordinary character. An S-corp election only matters if the activity is a business with self-employment income. This is general knowledge, not sourced here; confirm with counsel.
- Holding domains as "inventory" (dealer) means no capital gains rate, but it may allow inventory write-downs or abandonment losses on dropped names. Investors can generally claim a loss on abandonment of a capital asset in some circumstances. Professional advice is required.

### Gaps
- No current IRS ruling or case law specifically defining domain dealer status was retrieved (the Panavision v. Toeppen reference in forums was not verified).
- No authoritative source on US state sales tax for domain sales. Domains are generally not treated as tangible personal property, but some states tax digital goods or services; this is unverified.
- No authoritative source on whether Afternic, Sedo, or Atom act as EU VAT "deemed suppliers" or marketplace facilitators.
- Treatment of renewal fees for pure investors (deduct vs capitalize under §263(a) / §266 election) was not found in an authoritative source.

## .ai (Anguilla) registry risk

### Takeaway
.ai passed 1 million registrations in January 2026 and is now Anguilla's single largest revenue source: about 47.5% of government revenue in January–July 2026. Identity Digital has run the registry since January 2025 under a 5-year deal signed in October 2024. Wholesale price rose to $160 per mandatory 2-year term on 5 March 2026. The main risks are price escalation (the government depends on the revenue), concentration and hurricane exposure for a small island, and non-ICANN-contracted ccTLD rules that can change unilaterally.

### Cited Findings
- .ai passed 1,000,000 domains by about 2 January 2026 (Domain Name Stat), with about 2,080 new registrations per day in January 2026 — [Domain Name Wire, 2026-01-28](https://domainnamewire.com/2026/01/28/ai-namespace-hits-1-million-domain-names/); [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260127-anguilla-ai-domain-1-million)
- Revenue: EC$230M (US$85.3M) from .ai in 2025, exceeding the government's projections for 2025 through 2027 combined — [Anguilla Focus](https://anguillafocus.com/ai-domain-surge-brings-ec230m-windfall-to-anguilla-in-2025/)
- 2026: EC$217M (about US$80M) in January–July 2026, equal to 47.5% of all government revenue to date, per Premier Cora Richardson Hodge. The full-year expectation is EC$253M — [Jamaica Gleaner, 2026-10-09](https://jamaica-gleaner.com/index%2Ephp/article/business/20261009/anguillas-ai-domain-registration-earns-us80m-year-date-more-all-taxes)
- Conflicting projections: Sherwood cites a budget forecast of EC$260.5M (about US$96.4M), and others cite about US$70M. Figures differ by source and by what they measure — [Sherwood](https://www.sherwood.news/tech/now-more-one-million-ai-websites-contributing-an-estimated-70-million-anguilla-government-revenue/)
- In 2023, .ai provided about 20% of government revenue — [Domain Incite / Anguilla Focus via search](https://anguillafocus.com/us-company-signs-five-year-deal-to-manage-anguillas-ai-domains/)
- Identity Digital signed a 5-year agreement (MOU) with the Government of Anguilla in October 2024 and migrated the registry in January 2025 with "no downtime". Identity Digital reportedly keeps about 10% of sales — [Domain Name Wire, 2024-10-15](https://domainnamewire.com/2024/10/15/identity-digital-inks-ai-domain-deal-with-anguilla/); [Anguilla Focus](https://anguillafocus.com/us-company-signs-five-year-deal-to-manage-anguillas-ai-domains/); [Domain Incite](https://domainincite.com/30828-ai-channel-doubles-under-new-management)
- Pricing: from 5 March 2026, the wholesale fee rose by $10 per year to $160 per 2-year registration or renewal, up from $140 ($70/yr), an increase of about 14%. The increase applies to registration, renewal, transfer, and reactivation. .ai enforces a 2-year minimum at registration and at every renewal — [Domain Name Wire, 2026-02-02](https://domainnamewire.com/2026/02/02/ai-domain-name-prices-going-up-20/); [NameSilo](https://www.namesilo.com/blog/en/domain-name-search/ai-domain-registry-rules-pricing-and-renewals); [Namecheap](https://www.namecheap.com/blog/why-are-ai-domains-more-expensive/)
- Retail example (unverified forum repost): Namecheap renewal rising from $92.98 to $114.98 per year — [HN repost](https://hn.nuxt.dev/item/46883324)
- Concentration risk: commentary notes that resting the whole operation on one small island is a significant risk — [Domain Incite via search](https://domainincite.com/?p=30434)

### Inferences
- Holding cost for a 100-name .ai portfolio is now roughly $8,000+ per year at wholesale, or about $10–12K at retail, and must be paid 2 years at a time. That raises capital lock-up and drop-decision stakes.
- Because .ai funds about half of Anguilla's budget, there is a strong fiscal incentive for further price rises. Under the 5-year Identity Digital deal (expiring about October 2029), contract renewal or renegotiation is a 2029 event risk.
- Natural-disaster risk (Atlantic hurricanes, e.g., Irma in 2017 hit Anguilla) is now mitigated operationally because registry infrastructure runs on Identity Digital's systems, mostly off-island. Political and policy risk (pricing, eligibility rules) remains with the Anguillan government. This is inferred; no source explicitly addressed disaster recovery.

## .io (British Indian Ocean Territory) and the Chagos agreement

### Takeaway
The UK–Mauritius treaty was signed on 22 May 2025, but as of the latest reporting (April–July 2026) it is frozen and unratified. The US has withheld formal consent and the UK ran out of parliamentary time. .io operates normally. The legal trigger for retirement is removal of "IO" from ISO 3166-1, not the treaty itself. If that happens, ICANN/IANA policy gives a 5-year phase-out, extendable to up to 10 years. Identity Digital (about $40M+/yr from .io) has positioned itself with Mauritius by taking over the back end of .mu.

### Cited Findings
- The UK–Mauritius treaty transferring sovereignty over the Chagos Archipelago was signed on 22 May 2025 — [Domain Incite](https://domainincite.com/31642-io-safe-for-now-as-trump-puts-chagos-deal-on-ice)
- April 2026: the UK froze its plans because the Trump administration had not given formal written consent, and the government ran out of time to legislate in the current parliamentary session. Ministers deny the deal is dead — [Domain Incite, "io safe for now as Trump puts Chagos deal on ice"](https://domainincite.com/31642-io-safe-for-now-as-trump-puts-chagos-deal-on-ice)
- As of July 2026, .io was fully operational, "IO" was still in ISO 3166-1, and no retirement had started. The trigger would be deletion of the code from ISO 3166-1, followed by a 5-year runway extendable once (to up to 10 years) — [webhosting.today, 2026-07-02](https://webhosting.today/2026/07/02/you-dont-own-your-io-or-ai-you-rent-a-countrys-politics/); [NewName.ai blog](https://newname.ai/blog/io-domain-guide); [CircleID](https://circleid.com/posts/future-of-dot-io-domain-uncertain-as-uk-relinquishes-chagos-islands)
- ICANN's 2024 statement said nothing changes for .io unless the code is removed from ISO 3166-1. Per a secondary source, the original ICANN page was not retrieved — [webhosting.today](https://webhosting.today/2026/07/02/you-dont-own-your-io-or-ai-you-rent-a-countrys-politics/)
- The .io registry is run by a UK-based Identity Digital subsidiary earning more than $40M per year. Identity Digital appears to have taken over the back end for Mauritius's .mu, potentially future-proofing .io — [Domain Incite](https://domainincite.com/31642-io-safe-for-now-as-trump-puts-chagos-deal-on-ice)
- A btw.media item claims ICANN made the Chagos Archipelago "eligible for its own ccTLD .io". It is undated and conflicts with other reporting, so it is unreliable — [btw.media](https://btw.media/?p=84829)
- Prediction-market interest exists (Manifold market on whether .io will be substantially affected) — [Manifold](https://manifold.markets/AndrewDonnellan/will-the-io-cctld-be-substantially)

### Inferences
- For .io holders, the near-term (2026–2027) risk is low. The tail risk is a long, multi-year phase-out that gradually lowers resale value, so liquidity risk appears before any technical risk. The main outcomes are: (a) the treaty stays frozen and the status quo holds; (b) ratification with "IO" kept or reassigned under Mauritius, possibly enabled by an Identity Digital–Mauritius relationship; (c) ISO removal followed by a 5–10 year sunset.
- Investors should avoid long prepaid .io renewals as a "hedge". Monitor the ISO 3166 Maintenance Agency, IANA, and UK FCDO statements.

### Gaps
- No reporting from September–October 2026 on the treaty's status was found. Current status should be rechecked (UK FCDO, Hansard, US State Department).
- ICANN's and IANA's own statements were not retrieved directly. The ccNSO retirement policy text (adopted in 2021 and approved by the Board in 2022) was not fetched.

## Other ccTLD risks and gTLD renewal price hikes

### Takeaway
ccTLDs are governed by national law and policy, not ICANN contracts. There are precedents for seizure (.ly in 2010), loss through war or disruption (.ly in 2011), and nationalization (.me in 2026). gTLD price risk is increasing: .com wholesale rises to $10.97 on 1 November 2026, Identity Digital raised prices on 226 TLDs on 6 October 2026, and .xyz/.monster/.quest and others rose on 25 August 2026. Premium-tier renewals and new-gTLD renewal hikes are the classic investor trap.

### Cited Findings
- .ly: the Libyan registry seized vb.ly in 2010 over content contrary to its interpretation of Sharia law, then reserved .ly domains of 4 letters or fewer for local entities — [TheDomains, 2010](https://thedomains.com/2010/10/08/days-after-vb-ly-was-seized-by-the-libyan-registry-they-issue-a-statement-warning-on-any-4-letter-domain); [DomainInvesting](https://domaininvesting.com/risks-in-buying-cctld-domains-example-of-ly-domain-name-taken-by-registry)
- Letter.ly lost its domain when it could not renew during the Libyan war (2011) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=291079)
- .me: in August 2026, Montenegro's government started creating a state-owned entity to manage .me, which is currently run by doMEn, a JV primarily controlled by GoDaddy and Identity Digital. In May 2026, the government sought a bigger revenue share — [Domain Incite, "Government moves to nationalize .me"](https://domainincite.com/31858-government-moves-to-nationalize-me); [Domain Incite](https://domainincite.com/?p=31745)
- .com: wholesale rises from $10.26 to $10.97 on 1 November 2026 (+$0.71, about 6.9%). Renewing before 1 November locks the current price for up to 10 years. The .com agreement allows up to 7% increases in each of the final 4 years of the pricing period; .net may rise up to 10% per year through mid-2029 — [Openprovider](https://www.openprovider.com/blog/com-price-increase-domain-resellers); [Openprovider](https://www.openprovider.com/blog/are-domain-prices-going-up) (secondary; Verisign notice not retrieved)
- Identity Digital: registry price increase on 6 October 2026 across 226 extensions, e.g., .academy from $37.26 to $43.47 — [Spaceship KB](https://www.spaceship.com/knowledgebase/price-update-on-226-identity-digital-domain-extensions/)
- Namecheap: increases effective 25 August 2026 for .xyz, .monster, .quest, .ceo, .game, .tickets and others — [Namecheap blog](https://www.namecheap.com/blog/price-increase-for-xyz-monster-quest-and-more-domains/)
- Historical precedent: Uniregistry announced increases of up to 3,000%+ on some gTLDs, then backtracked after investor backlash. Older Donuts increases did not apply to registry-reserved premium names, which have their own pricing — [cctld.ru / Domain Incite](https://cctld.ru/en/media/news/industry/36557); [Domain Incite](https://domainincite.com/tag/prices/page/2)

### Inferences
- New-gTLD registry agreements require 6 months' notice of renewal price increases but impose no cap. ccTLDs have no ICANN price controls at all. Investors should read each registry's premium renewal policy before buying: many premium new-gTLD names renew at the premium price every year, not the standard rate. The notice requirement is general knowledge, not verified in this search.
- .co is run by GoDaddy Registry under Colombia's government and .gg by Island Networks (Guernsey). No specific 2025–2026 incidents were found, but both face the same structural sovereign-policy risk.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 sources found on .co or .gg policy or price changes.
- Premium renewal terms for specific registries in 2026 were not retrieved.
- The primary Verisign or ICANN notice for the November 2026 .com increase was not retrieved.

## Escrow, theft, and fraud risks

### Takeaway
The main threats are registrar-account takeover (phishing, credential stuffing, social engineering of registrar support), scam buyers (fake escrow sites, transfer-before-payment, chargebacks), and registrar migrations with weak default authentication (Squarespace, 2024). Standard defenses: licensed escrow (Escrow.com) or established marketplace payment flows, registrar and registry locks, hardware-key 2FA, and current recovery contacts.

### Cited Findings
- Domain hijacking usually happens through takeover of the registrar account via phishing, credential stuffing, or social engineering of support staff. Defenses are transfer lock, hardware-key 2FA, registry lock for high-value names, and current contact emails — [Namefi glossary](https://namefi.io/r/en/glossary/domain-hijacking)
- Common sale scams: fake escrow sites, fake buyers, overpayment or chargeback fraud, and pressure to transfer before payment — [Namefi domain-security topics](https://namefi.io/r/en/topics/domain-security?view=list)
- 2024 Squarespace migration (from Google Domains): attackers hijacked crypto and DeFi project domains because migrated accounts lacked strong default authentication — [Namefi](https://namefi.io/r/en/topics/domain-security?view=list)
- Buyers should check WHOIS history for recent contact changes, which can signal a stolen domain, and use escrow services (Escrow.com, Sedo, Afternic) — [DomainInvesting](https://domaininvesting.com/hijacking-domain-names-for/)
- Older reporting (WSJ via DomainInvesting) described web-address theft as an "everyday event"; eWeek documented forged-ID registrant changes — [DomainInvesting](https://domaininvesting.com/wsj-web-address-theft-is-everyday-event/); [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/security/domain-theft-is-still-a-little-too-easy/)

### Inferences
- Checklist: unique email for the registrar account; hardware-key or TOTP 2FA (not SMS); registrar lock on every name; registry lock (server-level) for high-value names; diversify across 2–3 reputable registrars; verify an escrow URL by typing it directly (fake escrow is the top scam); never transfer before the escrow confirms funds; be wary of buyers asking for "appraisal" fees. ICANN's 60-day post-transfer lock and Transfer Policy give some recovery leverage via the registrar. The checklist synthesizes the sources plus general practice.
- Stolen-domain purchase risk: buying a stolen name can lead to forced return without refund. Escrow plus WHOIS-history checks reduce, but do not eliminate, this risk.
