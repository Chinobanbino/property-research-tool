# Domain Investor Toolkit: Marketplaces, Sourcing, and Valuation (as of Oct 2026)

> Method note: All findings below come from web search results gathered 2026-10-21. Direct page fetches failed (DNS errors on domainnamewire.com, afternic.com, domaindetails.com, etemora.com, blog.afternic.com), so I could not read official pricing pages in full. Many figures come from search snippets and third-party/SEO guides. Each one carries a date where I could find one. Treat fee numbers as "verify on the official page before quoting."

## Sales marketplaces and landers: commissions, distribution, payment plans, current status

### Takeaway
GoDaddy's Afternic is now the main distribution channel. Dan.com shut down on 2025-06-27 and was folded into Afternic. Afternic's 2026 default commission is reportedly about 30%, falling to 15-20% depending on nameserver and plan choices, and lease-to-own (LTO) is now allowed on deals up to $5M. Sedo stays at 10-20%. Spaceship's SellerHub, the low-cost challenger, doubled its commission from 5% to 10% in Feb 2026. Escrow.com charges roughly 2-2.6% on smaller deals.

### Cited Findings
**Dan.com → Afternic**
- GoDaddy bought Dan.com in 2022 (reportedly for more than $71M). In Sept 2024 Dan closed new sign-ups and began migrating seller accounts to Afternic. Sellers could self-migrate, auto-migrate, or opt out, and opting out deleted their listings. — [Domain Name Wire, 2024-09-12](https://domainnamewire.com/2024/09/12/afternic-reveals-dan-com-migration-timeline-and-eventual-shutdown/); [DNW, 2024-09-18](https://domainnamewire.com/2024/09/18/dan-com-account-migration-is-now-available/); [DomainInvesting](https://domaininvesting.com/dan-retired-godaddy/)
- Dan.com shut down on 2025-06-27 and account access was disabled. dan.com now redirects to Afternic. — [DomainsDealer](https://domainsdealer.com/blog/what-happened-to-dan-com); [PounceDomains](https://pouncedomains.com/blog/dan-com-alternative)
- Existing Dan lease deals moved over in Nov 2024. Some buyer-side LTO arrangements were integrated into GoDaddy itself rather than Afternic. — [PounceDomains](https://pouncedomains.com/blog/dan-com-alternative); [DomainsDealer](https://domainsdealer.com/blog/what-happened-to-dan-com)
- Dan's "bring your own lead" feature survives as Afternic's **Custom Checkout Link**: 5% on a Buy Now sale, $15 minimum commission, $99 minimum price (reported as of 2026-09-07). — third-party summary via [PounceDomains](https://pouncedomains.com/blog/dan-com-alternative) / [DomainsDealer](https://domainsdealer.com/blog/what-happened-to-dan-com) (not confirmed on Afternic's page)

**Afternic (GoDaddy)**
- One source checked Afternic's pricing page in Sept 2026 and reports a **default commission of 30%**: a 25% base plus an automatic 5% "Boost" component. Pointing nameservers to GoDaddy Aftermarket lowers it to **20%**, and also downgrading to the "Basic" plan lowers it to **15%**. — [domaindetails.com platforms comparison (2026)](https://domaindetails.com/kb/domain-investing/domain-aftermarket-platforms-compared)
- Older or alternative figures conflict: "15% with GoDaddy aftermarket nameservers, 25% otherwise, $15 minimum" ([DomainsDealer](https://domainsdealer.com/blog/what-happened-to-dan-com)); "15-30%" ([wpdomainforsale](https://wpdomainforsale.com/sedo-vs-afternic-vs-dan-com/)); "~20% for Fast Transfer" ([names.center](https://names.center/blog/afternic-domain-marketplace-guide.html)). The 30%/20%/15% structure is the most recent figure, but it comes from a single third-party source.
- **Fast Transfer**: when a domain is enabled for Fast Transfer, Afternic distributes the listing across GoDaddy's registrar network and partner registrars, so buyers can purchase it instantly from registrar search results. — [names.center guide (2026)](https://names.center/blog/afternic-domain-marketplace-guide.html)
- **LTO**: At ICA 2026, GoDaddy Aftermarket GM Alan Shiflett announced that the maximum LTO price rises from **$100,000 to $5 million**. Afternic also added bulk LTO edits from the Portfolio view and "Good as Gold" payouts. — [Afternic blog, Q1 2026](https://blog.afternic.com/whats-new-q1-2026/)
- LTO terms run up to 60 months. Longer terms earn a commission discount: 0 points for 2-12 months, 5 points for 13-24, 10 points for 25-36, and 15 points for 37-60 (one example: a 36-month lease reduces a 30% default to 20%). — [Afternic LTO guide](https://blog.afternic.com/lease-to-own-guide/); [PounceDomains](https://pouncedomains.com/blog/dan-com-alternative)

**Sedo**
- 15% on Buy Now, Make Offer, or auction sales. Fixed-price parked domains with Sedo nameservers that sell on Sedo pay 10%. Sales through SedoMLS partner sites pay **20%**. Commissions exclude VAT. — [Sedo "It pays to sell at Sedo"](https://sedo.com/us/sell-domains/it-pays-to-sell-at-sedo); [NamePros thread with Sedo reply (2023)](https://www.namepros.com/threads/sedo-forcing-commission-payment.1128058/)
- Sedo's transfer/escrow service for off-platform deals costs 3% of the gross price, with a minimum of $60 (category 1) or $200 (category 2). — [Sedo](https://sedo.com/us/sedo/farewell-minimum-fee) (search snippet; page date unclear)
- I found no official 2026 Sedo price list. The 10/15/20 structure appears stable over several years.

**Atom (formerly Squadhelp)**
- I found no 2026 commission schedule in search results.
- The Aug 2024 "Commission Match Guarantee" pays sellers 10% of the Atom BIN price (capped at $300) when a premium listing sells on another approved platform. Eligibility requires Silver, Gold, or Platinum status (50+ premium listings), a BIN outright sale, and an Atom price at least 10% below the external BIN for the prior 30 days. Choosing this program ends any Reduced Commission benefit. — [TLDInvestors, 2024-08](https://tldinvestors.com/2024/08/atom-to-offer-commission-match-guarantee.html); [NamePros](https://www.namepros.com/threads/atom-com-launches-commission-match-guarantee.1332500/)
- A Mar 2026 Trustpilot review complains that unlisting a premium listing needs 30 days' notice through support. Atom replied that premium listings are an optional listing type. — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/atom.com?page=5) (single unverified review)

**Spaceship SellerHub (Namecheap's sister company)**
- In Feb 2026 Spaceship raised the SellerHub commission from **5% to 10%** and rolled back a test that had charged buyers 5%. Checkout and self-serve links stay at 5%. During negotiation, sellers can split the fee with the buyer (e.g. $10/$10 on a $200 sale), including on LTO deals. — [DNW, 2026-02-11](https://domainnamewire.com/2026/02/11/spaceship-increasing-sellerhub-commission-to-10/); [Spaceship SellerHub FAQ](https://www.spaceship.com/en-GB/sellerhub/)
- DNW commentary from Feb 2026 called 5% domain commissions "not sustainable." — [DNW, 2026-02-18](https://domainnamewire.com/2026/02/18/five-percent-domain-commissions-arent-sustainable/)
- Before the change, Spaceship said sellers had saved more than $1M in commissions. — [DomainInvesting](https://domaininvesting.com/spaceship-touts-over-1m-in-commission-savings/)
- Older Spaceship blog posts still say "always 5%" and are now out of date. — [Spaceship blog](https://www.spaceship.com/blog/sellerhub-domain-marketplace-review/)

**Escrow.com**
- 2026 sources describe a tiered schedule adopted in 2024: about 2.6% up to $5k ($50 minimum), 2.4% up to $50k, and 1.9% up to $200k. The fee can go to the buyer, the seller, or split 50/50. — [DomainsDealer](https://domainsdealer.com/blog/how-much-does-domain-escrow-cost); [WhatIsEscrow (verified Aug 2026)](https://whatisescrow.com/online-escrow-services)
- Other sources conflict: one gives 3.25% up to $5k with a $10 minimum and a 3.45% credit-card surcharge. — [names.center 2026](https://names.center/domain-escrow-services-comparison-2026.html)
- The Domain Holding Service (used for installment plans) charges $85 per administrative change, with terms from 3 months to 5 years. — [Escrow.com FAQ](https://www.escrow.com/support/faqs/what-is-the-fee-for-the-domain-holding-service). Escrow.com installment sales are explained at [DomainSherpa](https://domainsherpa.com/buy-a-domain-name-over-time-in-installments-with-escrow-com/) (older).

### Inferences
- Afternic Fast Transfer is still close to essential for reaching registrar-search buyers. The cost of that reach went up in 2026: unless you point nameservers to GoDaddy and accept the Basic plan, the default is about 30%.
- After Dan closed, Spaceship SellerHub, Sedo, and self-hosted landers (e.g. Efty) are the main lower-commission alternatives. Spaceship's move to 10% suggests the "5% era" is over.
- On a $5k sale, the net varies widely: about $4,500 on Spaceship, about $4,250 on Sedo at 15%, and $3,500-$4,250 on Afternic depending on settings. A direct sale through Escrow.com nets about $4,870 if the seller pays the full fee.

### Gaps
- Efty and DomainAgents pricing: searches returned nothing current. Efty historically ran on a subscription model with no commission, and DomainAgents on a buyer-offer and commission model, but I have no 2026 sourced figures for either.
- Atom's current seller commission tiers (historically about 15-30%) are unconfirmed for 2026.
- I could not read the official Afternic pricing page directly, so the 30% default comes from a single third-party source.

## Registrars for cheap portfolio holding (.com renewals, Verisign increase, .ai/.io)

### Takeaway
On **2026-11-01** the Verisign .com wholesale price rises from $10.26 to **$10.97** (+$0.71, about 6.9%), putting the at-cost floor including the ICANN fee at about $11.17. Spaceship and Cloudflare were the cheapest .com renewals before the increase, at about $10-10.50. .ai renews at about $75-$180 per year depending on registrar, which makes .ai holding costs 7-15x those of .com.

### Cited Findings
- Verisign announced on 2026-04-23 (alongside Q1 earnings) that .com goes from $10.26 to $10.97 effective 2026-11-01 at 04:00 UTC. This is the first change since Sept 2024. — [BusinessWire/Verisign, 2026-04-23](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260423135311/en/); [OSIR](https://osir.com/en/blog/verisign-com-price-update-2026/); [Openprovider](https://www.openprovider.com/blog/com-price-increase-domain-resellers)
- Verisign is contractually allowed to raise .com prices in 2026. — [DNW, 2025-11-19](https://domainnamewire.com/2025/11/19/verisign-can-increase-com-prices-in-2026/)
- The ICANN fee ($0.20) comes on top of the wholesale price, putting the all-in floor at about $11.17 (up from $10.46). If every allowed increase is taken at the maximum, wholesale could reach about $13.42 by the end of the current contract; OSIR describes this as a ceiling, not a forecast. — [OSIR](https://osir.com/en/blog/verisign-com-price-update-2026/); [TLDES](https://tldes.com/pages/com-price-increase-2026)
- Renewals paid before Nov 1 are billed at the current price for the full term, up to 10 years. This makes multi-year pre-renewal a legitimate cost hedge. — [TLDES](https://tldes.com/pages/com-price-increase-2026)
- .net rises 10%, from $10.91 to $12.00, on 2027-03-01. — via [DNW 2026-08-27](https://domainnamewire.com/2026/08/27/verisign-announces-net-price-increase/) and search summary
- Pre-increase .com renewal prices in 2026 comparisons:
  - Spaceship: about $9.98-$10.18 (below wholesale).
  - Cloudflare: at cost, about $10.44-$10.46, but requires Cloudflare nameservers, which conflicts with marketplace landers or Afternic nameserver discounts.
  - Porkbun: about $10.67-$11.08.
  - Dynadot: about $10.88-$10.99.
  - NameSilo: $10.99-$11.95 (sources vary).
  — [domaindetails cheapest registrars](https://domaindetails.com/registrars/cheapest); [sidebysidedomains](https://sidebysidedomains.com/blog/cheapest-domain-registrar-renewals-2026.html); [tldprice.org](https://tldprice.org/); [instantdomainsearch](https://instantdomainsearch.com/learn/guides/best-domain-registrars-2026)
- Dynadot ran a .com transfer promotion at $10.49 (code COMSUMMER26, summer 2026). — [NamePros](https://www.namepros.com/threads/sedo-com-official-thread.408817/post-8935755)
- A search summary attributed a $19.99/yr .com price from Nov 1 to NameSilo, but the underlying source appears to be a Malaysian host's notice. **Likely misattributed, do not use.** — [ServerFreak notice](https://helpdesk.serverfreak.com/en/announcements/article/important-notice-com-domain-price-adjustment-effective-1-november-2026)
- .ai renewal prices from DomainOffer aggregator snapshots (May-Aug 2026):

  - PlanetHoster: $84.99.

### Inferences
- After Nov 1, expect the cheapest .com renewals to land at roughly $10.70-$11.50. Each $0.71 adds $710/yr to a 1,000-name portfolio, which pushes up the sell-through rate a portfolio needs to break even.
- For portfolios that sell through Afternic, the Cloudflare nameserver requirement is a real drawback. Spaceship or Porkbun keep nameserver flexibility at a similar cost.

### Gaps

## Sourcing: hand registration, expired auctions, drop-catching, wholesale, outreach, tools

### Takeaway
GoDaddy killed its backorder service in Oct 2025 but still runs expiry auctions. DropCatch (about $59 per backorder) and NameJet/SnapNames (about $69-79, sharing one pool) are the main catchers. Park.io (about $99 on success) covers hacker TLDs. Hand registrations only make sense at roughly a 1% annual sell-through rate and $2-3k prices.

### Cited Findings
- GoDaddy retired its backorder and monitoring service on 2025-10-07. GoDaddy Auctions still runs expiry auctions during the grace period. Membership is about $5/yr, and a Feb 2026 terms update reportedly removed some bidder protections. — [domaindetails expired auctions comparison (2026)](https://domaindetails.com/kb/domain-investing/expired-domain-auctions-comparison); [domainauctions.io](https://domainauctions.io/godaddy-backorders-are-dead-6-alternatives-that-work)
- DropCatch: about a $59 backorder deposit, refunded on failure (one guide quotes $13-59). Contested catches go to a public auction, and caught names are locked for 60 days. — [domaindetails](https://domaindetails.com/kb/domain-investing/expired-domain-auctions-comparison); [names.center](https://names.center/how-to-buy-an-expired-domain.html)
- NameJet/SnapNames: about $69-79 minimum. They share a single pool, so you should not backorder on both. NameJet runs private auctions. — [domaindetails](https://domaindetails.com/kb/domain-investing/expired-domain-auctions-comparison)
- Park.io charges about $99 on success and is suited to hacker TLDs (.io etc.). One guide says expired .ai names have moved to an official registry auction system, which I could not verify. — [expireddomains.ai guide](https://expireddomains.ai/buying-expired-domains-2026); [names.center](https://names.center/how-to-buy-an-expired-domain.html)
- Top services catch an estimated 30-50% of contested names (rough estimate). — [names.center](https://names.center/how-to-buy-an-expired-domain.html)
- Hand-registration economics:
  - A NamePros model assumes a $10 .com, about a 1% annual sell-through rate, and brandables priced around $2-3k, and finds this viable.
  - At a $200 price, quality names need about a 4.25% sell-through rate just to cover renewals.
  - Industry-wide .com sell-through is "almost certainly under 1%."
  — [NamePros "Domain Name Sell-Through Rates"](https://www.namepros.com/blog/domain-name-sell-through-rates.1164616/); [NamePros "Minimum Domain Price for Profitability"](https://www.namepros.com/blog/the-minimum-domain-price-for-profitability.1313195/); [NamePros thread](https://www.namepros.com/threads/sell-through-rates.1193687/)
- One investor's data shows sell-through falling as price rises: 1.5% at an average price of about $1,450, and 0.43% at about $4,681. A well-optimized portfolio sells about 1-1.5% per year (Namecheap). Name.com cites typically under 3%. — [NamePros thread](https://www.namepros.com/threads/sell-through-rates.1193687/); [Namecheap blog](https://www.namecheap.com/blog/economics-of-domain-investing/); [Name.com](https://www.name.com/blog/how-to-start-domain-investing)

### Inferences
- With renewals of about $11, a 1,000-name portfolio costs about $11k/yr. At a 1% sell-through rate (10 sales), the average net sale price must exceed $1,100 just to break even, before acquisition costs. That math favors fewer, better names bought at expiry auctions over bulk hand registration.

### Gaps
- I found no sourced 2026 data on:
  - ExpiredDomains.net, DomainIQ, or Reverse WHOIS tool pricing and features.
  - NamePros wholesale market price levels.
  - Closeout mechanics at GoDaddy or Dynadot.
  - Private-outreach acquisition success rates.

## Valuation: comps, automated appraisals, value factors, wholesale vs retail

### Takeaway
NameBio comparable sales are the most defensible anchor. GoValue and Estibot rank names reasonably against each other but are unreliable on individual prices, especially for brandables and new TLDs.

### Cited Findings
- GoDaddy's own study (about 2,000 Afternic sales from late 2017) claimed GoValue beat human experts and Estibot. Because GoDaddy evaluated its own model, this is not independent. — [GoDaddy blog](https://www.godaddy.com/resources/news/domain-name-valuation)
- A NamePros user test found GoValue overestimated values by about 2.4x but ranked names with moderate success (correlation 0.50). It mishandled new extensions, and the tester recommended NameBio comps instead. — [NamePros](https://www.namepros.com/threads/how-accurate-is-godaddy-appraisal-govalue.1065693/)
- A May 2026 DNW test reportedly found GoValue ranked names sensibly but got individual values badly wrong (e.g. a .ai appraised at $171 sold for $8,000). This is second-hand via a search summary and could not be fetched directly.
- In a small, self-interested 10-name test, GoValue undervalued brandables by an average of 58% and came within 30% on generic keyword names. — [Pearl Street Ventures](https://pearlstreetventures.com/blog/godaddy-appraisal-vs-estibot-accuracy)
- Estibot says it is "quite accurate on average," but individual appraisals vary. — [EstiBot About](https://www.estibot.com/about)
- The widely repeated "75-85% accuracy" claim for appraisal tools has no traceable study and should not be cited. — (assessment from the search review)
- Valuation factors listed in guides include length, keyword search volume and CPC, extension, end-user demand, and comparable sales. — [seo.domains valuation factors](https://seo.domains/seo-resources/domain-valuation/domain-valuation-factors-process/)

### Inferences
- In practice, investors use NameBio comps as the anchor, then check two things: (a) how many extensions the keyword is already registered in, as an end-user demand proxy, and (b) how many operating businesses use the term. Automated appraisals serve only as a sanity check or sorting tool.
- The wholesale-to-retail spread is commonly described in the industry as about 5-10x+ (retail end-user prices vs investor bids). I did not find a sourced figure, so this is a gap and not a finding.

### Gaps
- I found no sourced, quantified wholesale-vs-retail spread.
- I found no current NameBio subscription pricing, and no current Atom appraisal reliability data.

## Outbound selling: finding end users, cold email practices, effectiveness

### Takeaway
I found no domain-specific 2026 outbound data. General B2B cold email in 2026 averages about a 3.4% reply rate and about 0.2% conversion to a deal, which implies outbound domain sales are a high-volume, low-yield channel.

### Cited Findings
- 2026 average cold email reply rate is 3.43% (Instantly benchmark), with the top decile at 10.7% or more. Reply rates have declined from about 8.5% in 2019 to about 5% in 2025 to 3.43% in 2026. — [Autobound 2026 guide](https://www.autobound.ai/blog/cold-email-guide-2026); [LeadHaste benchmarks](https://leadhaste.com/blog/outbound-sales-benchmarks-2026)
- Cold email converts to a deal at about 0.2%, or roughly 1 deal per 464-500 emails. — [Reachoutly](https://reachoutly.com/cold-email/conversion-rate/); [Martal](https://martal.ca/b2b-cold-email-statistics-lb/)
- 58% of replies come from the first email and 42% from follow-ups. — [Snov.io](https://snov.io/blog/cold-email-statistics/)
- Deliverability practices: use secondary sending domains, set up SPF, DKIM, and DMARC, and warm up new mailboxes for about 3 weeks. — [Unify](https://www.unifygtm.com/explore/cold-email-2026-domain-setup-deliverability-sequences)

### Inferences
- Applied to domains, about 500 targeted emails per sale is plausible only when the end-user list is tight (e.g. companies already using the term on another TLD or with hyphens). Investors should find end users through the same signals used in valuation: the term registered in other TLDs, operating businesses with the name, and active advertisers.

### Gaps
- I found no sourced domain-industry outbound response or close rates, and no current guidance on anti-spam or legal limits (e.g. CAN-SPAM, GDPR) specific to domain outbound.
