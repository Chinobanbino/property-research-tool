## 3. Why each part of the last two pages is there

| What's on the page | Principle | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| "Be honest" checklist | Commitment & consistency, self-identification | Checking "I haven't made that call yet" is a small yes. People who say yes once tend to keep going. |
| "Nothing is wrong with you" | Fundamental attribution error, Liking | It blames the hold-music system, not the reader. No shame means no defensiveness. |
| "Every month a fee stays, you pay it again" | Loss aversion | Stating the ongoing loss moves people more than "save money." It's simply true. |
| "Two good options: DIY or us" | Autonomy, choice architecture | It respects the reader who bought a DIY book. Only two choices, nothing to weigh up. |
| "Same playbook, same scripts" | Consistency, trust transfer | The book already earned their trust, and FeeArmor rides on it. |
| 3 steps, starting with "snap a photo" | Activation energy, BJ Fogg (ability) | Step 1 takes 10 seconds. |
| "$0 upfront, no savings no fee" + "billed only after it shows on your bill" | Regret aversion, Zero-price effect, Trust | Nothing to pay until the savings are real. Billing after the bill posts answers the #1 complaint about bill services (fees on savings that never showed up). |
| "Save nothing, pay $0" | Regret aversion, Zero-price effect | Nothing to lose. |
| Reader perk + code RESCUE | Reciprocity, Unity ("reader"), Endowment | A gift only readers get. Having a code feels like owning something already. |
| QR + "about 60 seconds, no card" | Friction cut, Hyperbolic discounting | They can act right now from the couch. |
| "Never ask for passwords / never pretend to be you" | Risk reversal | It answers the #1 fear about bill services before they ask. |
| Signed note from Giovanni | Liking, Authority | A real person they just spent a book with. |
| The bill-select question on the form | Micro-commitment + segmentation | Naming "the bill that bugs me most" makes it personal, and tells us which call to pitch. |

**Ground rules followed:** no fake urgency or scarcity, no testimonials or savings numbers we don't have, the SMS box is unchecked and optional, competitors unnamed, every unconfirmed item in [CONFIRM] brackets.

## Calls I made
- **Two pages, not one:** the "be honest" page does the persuading, so the offer page can stay short and clean.
- **Last page is FeeArmor's dark brand:** the visual switch tells readers "this is the next step," not more of the book. If you sell printed copies, that page is ink-heavy; I can make a light version.
- **The form asks for 3 things** (name, email, worst bill), with phone optional and its own unchecked text-consent box (TCPA). Every extra required field costs signups.
- **Readers get their own page (/rescue),** hidden from Google. The QR codes carry the edition, so you'll see Gumroad vs. Kindle vs. Call Sheet signups.
- **No competitor comparison on the pages:** at 40% we match BillShark, so FeeArmor wins on how it bills (after savings post, split payments), not on price.
- **No savings numbers, testimonials or "most people" stats** anywhere.
