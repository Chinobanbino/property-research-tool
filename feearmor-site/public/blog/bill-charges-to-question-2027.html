10 Bill Charges to Question Before 2027 Arrives

By Ryan Forman · November 23, 2026

Quick answer

Thanksgiving week is a good time to pull out every monthly bill and look for ten charges: an expired promo credit, equipment rentals, a missing autopay discount, carrier "recovery" fees, broadcast TV and regional sports fees, late fees, repeated one-time charges, data overages, and estimated meter reads on utility bills. Some can be removed, some can be offset with a credit, and a few can only be avoided by changing plans. One call per bill is usually enough to find out which is which.

Why these charges keep growing

Many providers now raise your bill without touching your plan price. They do it through separate line items that sit outside the advertised rate. T-Mobile is a clear 2026 example: its Regulatory Programs & Telco Recovery Fee went from $3.99 to $4.49 per voice line in January 2026, then to $5.49 on October 12, 2026. On plans that bill fees separately, that increase applies even with a price-lock guarantee, because the lock covers the plan rate, not the fee.

TV bills work the same way. Since December 2024, FCC rules require cable and satellite companies to show an "all-in" price for video service on bills and in ads. That makes the total easier to see, but it does not cap or remove the programming fees inside it.

The takeaway: the charges are legal and disclosed, but nobody is going to point them out to you. You have to look.

How to check your bills, step by step

  1. Gather two months of every bill. Internet, phone, TV, electric and gas. Download the full PDF, not the app summary.
  2. Put them side by side. Circle any line that is new, bigger, or missing compared with last month.
  3. Check credits first. A promo or loyalty credit shows as a negative number. If it disappeared, your promo likely ended. See what to do before your internet promo ends.
  4. Find equipment lines. Look for "Gateway," "Modem," "Router" or "Equipment." Our modem rental guide explains when you can drop it.
  5. Read the "Fees" or "Surcharges" section. On phone bills, this is where recovery fees live. On TV bills, look for broadcast TV and regional sports fees.
  6. Scan one-time charges. Installation, service visits and late fees should not repeat. If they do, ask for a credit.
  7. On utility bills, check the meter read type. If it says "estimated" (often marked "E"), compare it with your actual meter.
  8. Write down your questions before you call, so you ask about every line in one call.

What to say: a call script

You: Hi, I'm reviewing my bill before the new year and I have a few questions. Can you connect me with the retention or loyalty team?
Rep: Sure, one moment.
You: Thanks. My bill went from [last month] to [this month]. I see three things: my promo credit is gone, there's an equipment charge, and a [fee name] went up. Can we go through each one?
Rep: The promo ended on [date].
You: I understand. I've seen [competing provider] offer [service] for [price] at my address. I'd rather stay. What loyalty rate or credit can you apply so my total is closer to that?
You: I also see a [late fee / repeated charge] from [date]. I'd like that credited. And can you confirm my autopay discount is active? If it wasn't, please credit it back to when it should have started.
You: Great. Please email me the new monthly total and how long it lasts.

FAQ

Are recovery fees taxes? No. T-Mobile itself describes its fee as a company charge to recover its own costs, not a government tax. Taxes are listed separately.

Does a price lock stop these increases? Usually not. Price locks typically cover the plan rate only. Separately billed fees can still go up.

Can I get a late fee waived? Often, especially if it's your first. Ask politely for a one-time courtesy credit, and set up autopay so it doesn't happen again.

How do I know if my meter read was estimated? Your utility bill usually labels the read as actual or estimated. If it's estimated, you can ask the utility how to submit your own reading.

Is it worth calling for small amounts? Yes. A few dollars a month on several bills adds up to hundreds a year.

Bottom line

Most of the money leaking out of your bills hides in a handful of line items. Check these ten before 2027 and you start the year paying only for what you actually use.
