Utility Deposit Refund: How to Get Your Money Back

By Ryan Forman · November 20, 2026

Quick answer

If you paid a deposit to start electric or gas service, it isn't a fee — it's your money, and in many cases you can get it back once you've paid on time for a set period. Rules are set by each state's utility commission. In Texas, for example, a retail electric provider must refund a residential deposit after 12 consecutive bills paid with no late payments, and it must pay interest if it kept the deposit longer than 30 days. Check your bill for a "deposit held" line, then call and ask.

Why utilities charge deposits

A deposit protects the company if a new customer doesn't pay. It's usually requested when you start service without a payment history the company accepts, or after late payments or a disconnection.

Because deposits are regulated, companies can't keep them forever. In Texas, the Public Utility Commission (PUC) rules for retail electric providers say the deposit must be refunded after 12 consecutive residential billings with no late payments, and the refund can be applied as a credit on your bill. The same rules cap the deposit for residential customers at the greater of one-fifth of your estimated annual bill or the estimated bills for your next two months. Texas utilities outside the competitive market follow a separate rule with a lower cap, one-sixth of the estimated annual billing.

The problem: a refund doesn't always happen on its own, and many people forget they paid one. That's money sitting on someone else's books.

How to check whether you're owed a refund

  1. Find the deposit on your bill or account. Look for "Deposit," "Deposit held," "Security deposit" or "Deposit on file." Some providers show it only in the online account under billing details.
  2. Note the date and amount. Your receipt or first bill should show both. In Texas, providers must keep records of each deposit, including the amount and date.
  3. Count your on-time payments since then. Use your payment history. One late payment can restart the clock under some rules.
  4. Check for interest. In Texas, if a provider holds a deposit more than 30 days, interest is owed from the date of the deposit, at a rate the PUC sets each year. Interest is paid when the deposit is returned or credited, or yearly if you ask.
  5. If you moved or switched providers, check your final bill. In Texas, when you leave a retail electric provider, it must either transfer your deposit plus interest to your new provider or refund it, minus anything you still owe.
  6. If you're 65 or older, ask about exemptions. Texas rules let residential applicants 65 or older who aren't currently behind on any electric account establish credit without a deposit. Victims of family violence with a certification letter can also qualify.

What to say: a call script

You: Hi, I'd like to check on the deposit on my account.
Rep: Sure, what's your account number?
You: [Account number]. I paid a deposit of [amount] on [date]. Since then I've paid [number] bills on time. Do I qualify for a refund?
Rep: Let me check.
You: My understanding is that the rules require a refund after 12 on-time bills, with interest if you've held it more than 30 days. Can you apply it to my account or send it to me?
Rep: We can credit it to your next bill.
You: That works. Please confirm the amount, including interest, and the date it will post. Can you also note it on my account?

If they say you don't qualify: "Can you tell me which payment disqualified me and on what date? And when will I qualify?"

If you moved or switched: "My service ended on [date]. Was my deposit refunded or transferred, and where did it go?"

FAQ

Will my deposit be refunded automatically? Sometimes, but don't count on it. Check your bill after 12 on-time payments and call if you don't see a credit.

Do I get interest on my deposit? In Texas, yes, if the company kept it more than 30 days. Other states set their own rules.

What happens to my deposit when I move? In Texas, moving to a new address with the same regulated utility isn't treated as a disconnection, so no additional deposit can be required. If you leave a retail electric provider, it must transfer or refund your deposit, minus any balance owed.

Can they ask for a new deposit later? In Texas, a retail electric provider can't require an initial deposit from an existing customer unless that customer paid late more than once in the last 12 months or was disconnected for nonpayment.

I forgot about an old deposit. Is it gone? Not necessarily. Texas providers must keep records of unclaimed deposits for at least four years and make a reasonable effort to return them. Call your old provider first.

Bottom line

A utility deposit is a refund waiting to happen. Find it on your bill, count your on-time payments and ask for it back with interest.
