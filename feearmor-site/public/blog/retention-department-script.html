Retention Department Script: What to Say, Word for Word

By Giovanni Morado · November 13, 2026

Quick answer

Call your provider, say you're thinking about canceling, and ask for the retention (or "cancellations") department. Have three things ready: your current bill, the date your promo ended, and a real competing offer. Ask for a lower monthly rate, removal of junk fees, and a credit back to the date any billing error started. Stay polite, stay specific, and don't accept the first "no." The full script is below.

Why retention departments exist

Keeping a customer is usually cheaper for a provider than winning a new one. That's why internet, cable, satellite TV and phone companies have a team whose whole job is to stop cancellations. Those reps often have access to offers the general billing line won't mention: loyalty discounts, a new promo rate, waived equipment fees or one-time credits.

You don't need to be rude or bluff. You need to sound like a customer who has done the math and has somewhere else to go.

How to prepare (10 minutes)

  1. Check your bill line by line. Circle every charge that isn't the base plan: equipment rental, "network" or "broadcast TV" fees, late fees, and anything you don't recognize. Internet providers have been required since 2024 to show a "broadband facts" label with the monthly price and fees, so compare your bill to the label for your plan.
  2. Note your promo end date. Most big jumps happen when an introductory rate expires. Find the month your price went up. If you're not sure, see our guide to what to do when your internet promo ends.
  3. Find one real competing offer. Look up what another provider at your address charges for similar speed or channels. Write down the name, the price, the speed and whether it's a promo price. A real offer at your actual address is far more convincing than "I heard it's cheaper."
  4. Pick a good time to call. Call when you have 30 minutes and aren't rushed. Weekday mornings are often less busy than evenings and weekends. Have your account number and the last four digits of the account holder's SSN or your PIN ready.

The script, word for word

Opening

You: Hi, I'm calling about my bill. I've been a customer for [X] years, but my price went up to [$X] and I'm thinking about canceling. Can you transfer me to retention, or the cancellations department?

If the rep tries to help first:

Rep: I can look at that for you.
You: Thanks. I'd like to speak with the team that handles cancellations and retention offers, please.

Explain the problem

You: My promo ended in [month], and my bill went from [$X] to [$X]. I also see [$X] for modem rental and [$X] in other fees. That's more than I can justify.

Cite the competing offer

You: [Provider] is offering [speed/package] at my address for [$X] a month. I'd rather stay with you if you can get close to that. What can you do?
Rep: I can offer [$X] off for 12 months.
You: Thank you. What would the total be with taxes and fees? And what does it go to after 12 months?

Ask for fee removal

You: Can you also remove the [modem rental / fee name]? I'd be happy to use my own equipment if that's the issue.

Ask for credits back to the error date

You: I also noticed [describe error: I was charged for a box I returned / I was billed twice] starting in [month]. Can you credit that back to [month] and tell me the total credit?
Rep: I can credit the last month.
You: The error started in [month], so I'd like it credited back to then. Can you check, or connect me with someone who can?

Lock it in

You: Great. Can you confirm the new monthly total, the length of the offer, any contract or early termination fee, and send me a confirmation by email or text? Can I get a reference number for this call?

If they say no

  • Ask once more, calmly: "I understand. Is there anything else available for long-time customers? A different plan, a loyalty discount, or a smaller speed tier?"
  • Ask for a supervisor: "Could I speak with a supervisor or someone with more options?"
  • End politely and try again later: "Thanks for checking. I'll think about it." A different rep on a different day may have different offers.
  • Be ready to switch. If the competing offer is real and better, switching is a legitimate outcome. Check the new provider's price after the promo, and return any rented equipment to avoid fees.

Example (hypothetical): You save $30/mo = $360/yr. Our 40% fee is $144. You keep $216 in year one and 100% after.

FAQs

What is the retention department? It's the team at a provider that handles cancellation requests and has offers designed to keep customers. Ask for "retention," "loyalty" or "cancellations."

Do I have to actually cancel to get a discount? No. Saying you're considering canceling is usually enough to reach the right team. Only cancel if you've confirmed a better option.

Will this work for Xfinity, Spectrum or AT&T? The approach works with most large internet, TV and phone providers, but offers vary by provider, location and plan. Results are never guaranteed.

How often can I call? Many people call when a promo ends, which is often every 12 or 24 months. Calling before the price jumps gives you the most leverage.

Bottom line

Retention reps have room to negotiate, but they rarely offer it unless you ask.
