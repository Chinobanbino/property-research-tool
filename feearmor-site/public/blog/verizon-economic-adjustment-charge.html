Understanding the Verizon Economic Adjustment Charge and Lowering Your Bill

By Ryan Forman · November 13, 2026

If you noticed an unexpected increase on your monthly bill from Verizon Wireless, you are not alone. The Verizon Economic Adjustment Charge was introduced to business accounts starting June 16, 2022, as an added monthly surcharge per line. While fees like this make it challenging to reduce wireless bill totals, understanding what you are paying for is the first step toward securing account credits or discounts.

Deep Dive: How the Fee Ends Up on Your Bill

Verizon added this surcharge specifically to help offset operating costs. The Verizon Economic Adjustment Charge applies exclusively to business accounts, adding $2.20 per smartphone/data device line and $0.98 per basic phone/tablet line. Consumer accounts see a different line item called the Administrative and Telco Recovery Charge, which costs about $3.78 per voice line and $3.97 per data-only line after a Sept 1 increase. (These consumer figures were reported by PhoneArena from a customer's bill and have not been confirmed by Verizon.) Verizon states these surcharges are Verizon charges, not taxes or government fees. Base plan prices did not change; the fees are added on top.

Additionally, Verizon hidden fees and standard service costs have shifted elsewhere on statements: the account activation fee rose from $35 to $40, while the paperless and autopay discount of up to $10 per line requires a linked bank account or Verizon Visa.

As a hypothetical example, a business account with 4 smartphone lines pays 4 lines x $2.20 x 12 months = $105.60 per year in surcharges. A consumer account with 2 voice lines pays 2 lines x $3.78 x 12 months = $90.72 per year in surcharges.

Copy-Pasteable DIY Negotiating Script (L.E.A.P.)

Follow this step-by-step dialogue script to ask for credits or loyalty discounts that help offset added surcharges:

You: I am looking directly at my bill statement under surcharges and see the Verizon Economic Adjustment Charge billed at $2.20 per line. Could you review this line item with me?
You: I understand this is a standard line item, but it increases my total costs. Please transfer me to your Retention or Customer Loyalty department to discuss my options.
You: Competing carriers offer fixed rates without added fees, and I am considering canceling my lines if we cannot lower my overall invoice.
You: Can you apply a recurring promotional credit or loyalty discount to my account to offset this fee without lowering my current service level?

Why DIY Negotiation Often Fails

Attempting to lower your bill on your own can be frustrating. Long customer service hold times can waste hours of your workday. Customer service agents may try to lock you into contract traps or force you into plan downgrades to drop your bill. Furthermore, language barriers or complex bill terminology can make it difficult to assert your rights effectively.
